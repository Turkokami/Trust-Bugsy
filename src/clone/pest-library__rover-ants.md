# Rover Ants

Pest Library

## Rover Ants

Brachymyrmex species

Some of the smallest ants you will ever see indoors — often mistaken for “those tiny ants by the sink.” Rover ants have exploded across South Florida and cluster around moisture in kitchens and bathrooms.

## How to identify them

Rover ants are minute — at or under a sixteenth of an inch — and range from pale yellowish to dark brown. Because of their size they are frequently confused with other tiny ants; a professional confirms the species. They are most often noticed as small clusters and short trails around sinks, tubs, and window sills.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Rover ants have become extremely common in Florida’s urban and suburban landscapes. They nest in moist soil, mulch, potted plants, and leaf litter outdoors, and in wall voids and around plumbing indoors. Winged reproductives (swarmers) are often seen at windows, which alarms homeowners who fear termites.

They are strongly tied to moisture and are attracted to sweets, which is why bathrooms and kitchen sink areas are hot spots. Colonies can be large, and populations build quickly in the warm season.

## Signs of an infestation

- Very tiny ants clustered around sinks, tubs, and drains
- Small winged ants (swarmers) appearing at windows and light fixtures
- Trails in bathrooms, kitchens, and around potted plants
- Activity that intensifies in humid weather

## Why they are a problem

Rover ants do not sting, bite, or damage the structure, but their numbers and attraction to living spaces make them a nuisance, and their swarmers are often mistaken for termites, causing unnecessary alarm. Their tie to moisture means they can also flag damp conditions worth addressing.

## How Bugsy’s treats rover ants

## Preventing rover ants

- Fix leaks and reduce humidity around sinks, tubs, and A/C lines
- Empty saucers under potted plants and avoid overwatering
- Keep mulch and damp organic debris away from the foundation
- Seal gaps around plumbing, window frames, and utility lines
- Wipe up moisture and store sweets sealed

- Size: 1/16 inch or less (1-2 mm)
- Color: Pale yellow to dark brown
- Bite or sting: No
- Nests: Moist soil, mulch, wall voids
- Attracted to: Moisture and sweets
- Peak activity: Warm, humid months
