## Why Fort Lauderdale stays under year-round pest pressure

Fort Lauderdale earned its nickname — the Venice of America — from more than 300 miles of canals winding between the Intracoastal, the New River, and the Atlantic. That water is the city’s beauty and its year-round pest engine: canals, mangroves, and tidal flats breed no-see-ums and mosquitoes in volume, and every seawall is potential iguana habitat.

On land, the subtropical heat, dense tropical landscaping, and older urban core keep roof rats, palmetto bugs, and termites active in every season. Whether you’re on a finger isle, a historic downtown street, or a beachside condo, the right plan is one built for your exact setting.

## The pests that define Fort Lauderdale

### No-See-Ums & Mosquitoes

300+ miles of canals, the Intracoastal, and mangrove edges breed biting midges and mosquitoes that swarm docks, patios, and pool decks at dawn and dusk.

### Iguanas & Wildlife

Seawalls and canal banks across Fort Lauderdale are prime iguana habitat — they burrow, damage landscaping, and foul docks and pools.

### Roof Rats

Mature tropical landscaping and canopy give roof rats a direct route into attics, soffits, and outdoor kitchens.

### Palmetto Bugs & Termites

American roaches push up from sewers and drains after rain, while subterranean termites attack from the warm, moist soil.
