## Why termites love Delray Beach

Termites need warmth, moisture, and wood — and Delray Beach offers all three year-round. Our humid, frost-free climate lets colonies stay active every month of the year, and the city’s mix of historic wood-frame homes in Lake Ida and Del-Ida Park, waterfront properties along the Intracoastal, and slab-built homes to the west gives termites countless ways in.

There are two very different threats here, and they’re treated differently. **Subterranean termites** (including aggressive Formosan termites) live in the soil and enter through foundations, slabs, and cracks — often invisibly. **Drywood termites** live entirely inside dry structural wood, framing, and attics, with no soil contact at all. Spring is peak swarm season, when both send out winged reproductives to start new colonies — and when many homeowners first discover they have a problem.

## The two termites we treat in Delray Beach

Correct identification drives the right treatment — they are not the same job.

### Subterranean & Formosan Termites

Soil-dwelling termites that enter from the ground through foundations and slabs, often building mud tubes. Formosan colonies are especially large and destructive. Learn more in our subterranean termite guide.

### Drywood Termites

Live entirely inside dry wood — framing, trim, attics, and furniture — especially common in Delray’s older homes. Signs include tiny pellet-like droppings (frass) and discarded wings.

### What are the signs of termites?
Watch for mud tubes on foundations (subterranean), tiny pellet-like droppings or “frass” (drywood), discarded wings near windows after a swarm, hollow-sounding or blistered wood, and sagging or buckling floors and trim.

### What’s the difference between subterranean and drywood termites?
Subterranean termites live in the soil and enter from the ground; drywood termites live entirely inside dry wood with no soil contact. They require completely different treatments, which is why correct identification matters.
