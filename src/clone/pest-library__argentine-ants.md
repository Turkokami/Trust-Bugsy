# Argentine Ants

Pest Library

## Argentine Ants

Linepithema humile

A light-brown trailing ant that forms cooperative supercolonies, farms garden pests for honeydew, and pushes indoors in long, orderly lines during heavy rain or drought.

## How to identify them

Argentine ants are small, uniformly light-to-medium brown, and lack a stinger. When crushed they give off a faint musty odor. Colonies are recognized less by the individual ant than by the classic sign: distinct, well-defined foraging trails moving steadily between a nest and a food source.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Like several of Florida’s worst nuisance ants, Argentine ants form multi-queen supercolonies that cooperate rather than compete, allowing populations to reach very high densities. Nests are shallow and typically outdoors — under mulch, landscape timbers, stones, potted plants, and along foundations.

They are strongly attracted to sweets and tend aphids, scale, and mealybugs on plants for honeydew. Weather drives them indoors: heavy rain floods nests and drought dries up outdoor moisture, and in both cases trails push into kitchens and bathrooms in search of food and water.

## Signs of an infestation

- Long, steady trails of small brown ants along baseboards, counters, and foundation lines
- Ants appearing indoors right after heavy rain or during dry spells
- Nests found under mulch, pots, stones, and landscape edging
- Aphid and scale activity on nearby ornamental plants

## Why they are a problem

Argentine ants do not bite, sting, or damage structures, but their numbers and persistence make them a serious contamination nuisance in kitchens and pantries. Their supercolony structure means a treated area is quickly recolonized from neighboring nests unless control addresses the broader population.

## How Bugsy’s treats Argentine ants

Because spraying visible trails only splinters the colony, we use a non-repellent exterior perimeter product foragers transfer back to the nest, paired with targeted treatment of harborage under mulch, timbers, and pots. We trace trails to entry points, seal them, and schedule follow-up to keep neighboring supercolonies from re-invading.

## Preventing Argentine ants

- Pull mulch, stones, and potted plants back from the foundation
- Trim vegetation touching the structure
- Manage aphids and scale on landscaping to remove their honeydew supply
- Seal cracks, utility penetrations, and gaps around doors and windows
- Keep counters clean and store sweets in sealed containers
