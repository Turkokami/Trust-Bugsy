## Why spiders settle into Delray Beach homes

Spiders go where the food is — and in Delray Beach, warm nights, exterior lighting, and dense landscaping produce a steady supply of the insects they hunt. That draws them to eaves, screen enclosures (lanais), garages, sheds, and the undersides of patio furniture and railings, where they spin webs and lay egg sacs.

Most are harmless, but two matter: the **black widow** and the increasingly common **brown widow**, whose venom is medically significant. Both hide in dark, undisturbed, protected spots — garages, meter and utility boxes, under grills and furniture, and in stored items — exactly the places people reach into without looking. The brown widow, now widespread across South Florida yards, leaves distinctive spiky egg sacs that fuel fast population growth.

### What’s the difference between a black widow and a brown widow?
Black widows are glossy black with a red hourglass; brown widows are tan with an orange hourglass and spiky egg sacs. Both are venomous, though the brown widow’s bite is usually milder. The brown widow is now the more common of the two across South Florida.
