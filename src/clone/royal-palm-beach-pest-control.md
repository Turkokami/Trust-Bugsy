## Why Royal Palm Beach homes stay under year-round pressure

Royal Palm Beach was built around water. The village is threaded with man-made lakes, drainage canals, and the M-Canal along its western edge, and it backs onto the preserves and wetlands that run toward the Everglades. That water is what makes the community beautiful — and it is also a year-round breeding engine for mosquitoes, midges, and the moisture-loving pests that follow them indoors.

Add South Florida’s subtropical heat and a nine-month wet season, and pests here never really get a winter break. A ghost-ant trail on the counter, palmetto bugs in the garage after a downpour, or a rodent in the wall as the nights cool — in RPB these aren’t one-off surprises, they’re seasonal patterns. The fix is a plan tuned to exactly where your home sits.

## The pests that define Royal Palm Beach

Different lots face different pressure. Homes on a lake, canal, or preserve edge see the heaviest mosquito and wildlife activity; interior streets battle ants and roaches; and every home in the village is on the menu for subterranean termites.

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

The lakes, canals, and preserves that make RPB scenic are ideal breeding grounds. Mosquitoes and biting midges (no-see-ums) swarm at dawn and dusk, especially on waterfront and preserve-edge lots.

### Ants

Ghost ants, white-footed ants, and Caribbean crazy ants build huge multi-queen colonies in the moist landscaping around RPB homes, then trail indoors to kitchens and baths by the thousands.

### Palmetto Bugs & Roaches

American roaches (palmetto bugs) push in from sewers, mulch, and canals after heavy rain; German roaches ride in and breed fast in kitchens. Both surge through the wet season.

### Rodents, Termites & Wildlife

Roof rats travel the tree canopy into attics, subterranean termites attack from the soil, and preserve edges bring raccoons, opossums, and iguanas to western communities.
