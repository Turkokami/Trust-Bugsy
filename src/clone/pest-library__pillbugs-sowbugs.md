# Pillbugs & Sowbugs

Pest Library

## Pillbugs & Sowbugs

Order Isopoda (“roly-polies”)

The little gray armored “roly-polies” under every flower pot and stone — not insects at all, but land crustaceans that wander in from damp ground and quickly dry out and die indoors.

## How to identify them

Pillbugs and sowbugs are small, gray, segmented, armored-looking creatures with seven pairs of legs. Pillbugs (“roly-polies”) roll into a tight ball when disturbed; sowbugs are similar but cannot roll up and have two small tail-like appendages. Both are crustaceans — more closely related to shrimp than to insects — and both need constant moisture.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

These isopods live in damp, dark places — under mulch, stones, flower pots, logs, and leaf litter — where they feed on decaying plant matter and play a useful role in breaking down organic material. They breathe through gill-like structures and must stay moist to survive.

They wander indoors from a damp perimeter, especially where mulch or ground cover meets the foundation, turning up in garages, bathrooms, and along baseboards. The good news: indoor air is too dry for them, so those that get inside dry out and die within a day or two — they cannot establish indoors.

## Signs of an infestation

- Gray, segmented “roly-polies” under pots, mulch, and stones
- Dead or slow-moving pillbugs along garage and foundation walls indoors
- Activity where damp mulch or ground cover meets the house
- More sightings in wet weather

## Why they are a problem

Pillbugs and sowbugs do not bite, sting, spread disease, or damage sound structures — indoors they are strictly a nuisance, and a self-limiting one since they die in dry conditions. Outdoors, very large populations can occasionally nibble seedlings and soft fruit, but mostly they are harmless decomposers.

## How Bugsy’s treats pillbugs and sowbugs

Since these pests live and breed in the damp perimeter, control is about that zone: our technicians treat mulch beds, foundation edges, and ground cover, apply a targeted exterior barrier, and seal the ground-level gaps they wander through. Reducing moisture and harborage against the house is the lasting fix.

## Preventing pillbugs and sowbugs

- Keep mulch and ground cover pulled back from the foundation
- Improve drainage and reduce damp harborage near the house
- Seal gaps under doors, garage thresholds, and along the foundation
- Move pots, stones, and wood off the ground and away from walls
- Water landscaping in the morning so beds are not damp overnight

## At a glance

- Land crustacean
- Moisture-bound
- Harmless
- Perimeter wanderer

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/4 to 1/2 inch
- Color: Slate gray to brownish
- Group: Crustaceans, not insects
- Where: Under mulch, pots, stones, logs
- Bite/damage: None
- Peak activity: Warm, damp conditions
