## Why estate lots out west see the most pressure

The Estates sit on Royal Palm Beach’s western, more rural edge — bigger lots, canals, pastures, and preserve borders. That open, water-rich land is prime habitat for mosquitoes and no-see-ums, fire ants in the turf, and wildlife like raccoons, opossums, iguanas, and snakes working the tree lines and canal banks.

Acreage homes also give rodents and termites more ground to approach from. A perimeter-focused plan tuned to a large lot — not a tract-home template — is what actually keeps them back.

## The pests estate lots face

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

Canals, ponds, and preserve edges breed biting insects in volume; large lots need targeted resting-area and standing-water treatment.

### Fire Ants & Yard Ants

Open turf and pasture edges are ideal for fire-ant mounds and nuisance ants that move toward the home.

### Rodents & Wildlife

Roof rats, plus raccoons, opossums, and iguanas from the canal banks and tree lines, look for a way into attics, sheds, and outbuildings.

### Termites & Palmetto Bugs

Subterranean termites attack from moist soil, while palmetto bugs push in from mulch and canals after every rain.
