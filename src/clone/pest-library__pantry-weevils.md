# Pantry Weevils

Pest Library

## Pantry Weevils

Sitophilus species (rice & granary weevils)

Tiny, snout-nosed beetles that develop *inside* whole grains, rice, and seeds — so they are often carried home already in the package and multiply unnoticed in the pantry.

## How to identify them

Pantry (grain) weevils are small, hard-bodied beetles, reddish-brown to nearly black, with a distinctive long snout curving down from the head. They are slow-moving and are usually found in and around stored grain products, sometimes wandering on pantry shelves and counters away from the infested food.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

These weevils specialize in whole grains and seeds. A female chews a tiny hole in a kernel of rice, wheat, corn, or a dried bean, lays an egg inside, and seals it — so the larva develops hidden within a single kernel, feeding until it emerges as an adult. This is why infestations are so often brought home unseen inside a bag of rice or grain.

In the warm pantry they reproduce continuously, and populations build in rice, pasta, whole grains, birdseed, and seeds. Because the immature stages are inside kernels, the food can be infested well before any beetles are visible.

## Signs of an infestation

- Small snout-nosed beetles in or around rice, grains, and pasta
- Kernels with tiny exit holes; grain that looks powdery or hollowed
- Beetles wandering on pantry shelves and counters
- A problem that reappears from an overlooked bag of grain or seed

## Why they are a problem

Pantry weevils contaminate and hollow out stored staples, forcing disposal of infested rice, grain, and seed products. Their habit of developing hidden inside kernels means they spread from package to package and persist until every source is found, making them a frustrating recurring pantry pest.

## How Bugsy’s treats pantry weevils

Our technicians inspect the pantry to identify every infested source — including seldom-used grains, birdseed, and decorative seed items — recommend disposal of affected products, and treat shelf cracks and voids where weevils harbor. Because the insects ride in inside kernels, source removal and sanitation are the heart of control, supported by monitoring.

## Preventing pantry weevils

- Store rice, grains, and seeds in airtight hard containers
- Buy grain products in quantities you will use quickly and rotate stock
- Freeze new grains or seeds for a few days to kill any hidden stages
- Clean shelf cracks and corners where spilled grain collects
- Discard old, forgotten, or hollow-looking grain products
