## Why La Mancha homes stay under pest pressure

La Mancha’s appeal — quiet gated streets, community lakes, and decades of mature landscaping — is also what keeps pests active year-round. The lakes breed mosquitoes and midges, and the lush, irrigated yards feed multi-queen ant colonies that trail indoors.

As an established community, many homes have settled just enough to open small gaps around soffits and slabs where ants, roaches, and rodents slip in. We treat the source and seal the entries rather than chasing trails.

## The pests La Mancha homes face

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

Community lakes and irrigation keep mosquitoes and biting midges breeding through the long wet season, worst on lakefront lots at dawn and dusk.

### Ghost & White-Footed Ants

Moist, mature landscaping fuels enormous colonies that trail into kitchens and baths in thin, persistent lines.

### Palmetto Bugs

American roaches move in from mulch, drains, and lake edges after rain, showing up in garages and lanais.

### Roof Rats & Termites

Mature trees give roof rats a path to attics; subterranean termites work up from the soil under established slabs.
