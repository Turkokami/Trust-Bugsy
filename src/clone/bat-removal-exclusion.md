## Why bats can’t just be trapped or sprayed

Bats are beneficial, protected animals — and handling them the wrong way is both harmful and against Florida law.

### Guano & odor

Accumulated bat droppings stain ceilings, produce a strong odor, and can support the growth of harmful fungus — a cleanup issue, not just a nuisance.

### Protected species

All bat species in Florida are protected. It is illegal to kill or harm them, and exclusions must follow state rules on timing and method.

### They come back

Bats return to the same roost season after season. Without proper exclusion and sealing, the colony re-establishes itself every year.

Florida law matters here

## Timing isn’t optional — it’s the law.

Under Florida wildlife rules, bats cannot be excluded during maternity season — April 16 through August 14 — when flightless pups are in the roost and would be trapped and die inside. Outside that window, exclusion must still be done humanely with one-way devices that let bats out but not back in. We plan every job around these rules so it’s done legally and correctly.

## What we do

A complete, humane bat program — get them out, keep them out, and clean it up.

### Inspection & roost mapping

We find every entry point and confirm where and how the colony is getting in — the key to an exclusion that actually works.

### One-way exclusion

We install one-way devices at the active openings so bats can exit but can’t return, clearing the roost without harming the animals.

### Sealing & proofing

Once the colony is out, we seal the exterior gaps, vents, and construction voids they used, so the roost can’t re-establish.

### Guano cleanup & sanitizing

We remove and clean up accumulated guano and sanitize the affected area, addressing the odor and contamination they left behind.
