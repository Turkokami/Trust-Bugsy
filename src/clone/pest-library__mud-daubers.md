# Mud Daubers

Pest Library

## Mud Daubers

Sceliphron & Trypoxylon species

Solitary, remarkably docile wasps that build those tube- and organ-pipe-shaped mud nests on walls and under overhangs — mostly a cosmetic nuisance, but their old nests invite other, less friendly wasps.

## How to identify them

Mud daubers are slender wasps, often with a long thread-like waist, and come in black, metallic blue, or black-and-yellow depending on species. The easiest ID is their handiwork: nests made of mud, shaped like clustered tubes or side-by-side “organ pipes,” plastered onto walls, eaves, ceilings of porches, sheds, and under overhangs.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Unlike social wasps, mud daubers are solitary — each female builds and provisions her own nest with no colony to defend. She stocks the mud cells with paralyzed spiders as food for her larvae, which makes mud daubers useful natural spider control.

Because there is no colony and no nest-guarding instinct, mud daubers are among the most docile wasps and very rarely sting, even when their nests are handled. The catch: once a mud dauber abandons a nest, other wasps — including more aggressive species — sometimes move into the empty cells.

## Signs of an infestation

- Mud tubes or organ-pipe nests on walls, eaves, and under porches
- Single wasps working mud onto a wall rather than a swarming group
- Old, weathered mud nests left on sheds, overhangs, and siding
- Spider parts or holes in abandoned mud cells

## Why they are a problem

Mud daubers pose little sting risk, so the issue is mostly cosmetic — unsightly mud nests staining walls and eaves — plus the possibility that abandoned nests get taken over by other wasps. In rare cases their nests in vents or equipment can cause blockages.

## How Bugsy’s treats mud daubers

Because mud daubers are docile and solitary, control is straightforward: our technicians remove the nests, clean the mud staining where possible, and treat surfaces and sheltered areas that attract repeat nest-building. We also clear abandoned nests that could otherwise be reoccupied by less friendly wasps.

## Preventing mud daubers

- Remove mud nests promptly and clean the surface so they are less likely to rebuild there
- Reduce spider populations around the home — their food source
- Seal or screen vents and equipment openings where nests cause blockages
- Keep exterior lighting managed to reduce the insects that draw spiders and wasps
- Repaint or seal weathered surfaces that attract nest-building

## At a glance

- Solitary
- Mud nests
- Very docile
- Preys on spiders

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/2 to 1 inch
- Color: Black, metallic blue, or black-and-yellow
- Nest: Mud tubes / organ-pipe shapes
- Where: Walls, eaves, overhangs, sheds
- Sting: Rarely — very docile
- Peak activity: Warm months
