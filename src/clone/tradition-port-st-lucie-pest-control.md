## Why Tradition homes need year-round coverage

Tradition is a large master-planned town in western Port St. Lucie, built around lakes, preserves, and canals with fresh landscaping. That water and habitat keeps mosquitoes and midges active through the long wet season, and preserve edges bring iguanas and wildlife toward the homes.

Fire ants work the turf, roof rats travel the canopy, and palmetto bugs push in after rain. Homes near the water or preserve need a plan built for that exposure.

## The pests Tradition homes face

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

Community lakes, canals, and the nearby Everglades edge breed mosquitoes and biting midges that swarm at dawn and dusk.

### Fire Ants & Ghost Ants

Open turf and irrigated landscaping feed fire-ant mounds and ghost-ant colonies that trail indoors.

### Palmetto Bugs

American roaches push up from sewers and drains after rain, turning up in garages, lanais, and kitchens.

### Iguanas & Roof Rats

Iguanas colonize lake banks and canals, while roof rats travel the mature canopy into attics.
