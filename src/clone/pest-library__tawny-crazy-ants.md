# Tawny Crazy Ants

Pest Library

## Tawny Crazy Ants

Nylanderia fulva

Named for their fast, erratic, seemingly directionless movement, tawny crazy ants form massive supercolonies that displace fire ants, swarm electrical equipment, and are notoriously difficult to control with ordinary baits.

## How to identify them

Tawny crazy ants are small and uniformly golden-to-reddish brown, covered in fine hairs. The giveaway is behavior: rather than marching in tidy lines, they move in rapid, jerky, unpredictable paths across surfaces — the “crazy” in the name.

They do not build mounds. Instead you see huge numbers of ants suddenly appear under objects, in mulch, and along foundations, often in staggering density.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

This is a supercolony species: neighboring nests do not fight, so populations merge into sprawling, interconnected colonies with countless queens and no single center to eliminate. Numbers can build to the point that the ground appears to move.

They have a well-documented attraction to electrical equipment — meter boxes, A/C units, pumps, and junction boxes — where they crowd in and cause shorts and failures. A dead ant releases an alarm chemical that draws more ants, compounding the problem.

Tawny crazy ants also displace red imported fire ants and other native insects, and they tend honeydew-producing pests on plants for sugar. They bite when handled but have no painful sting.

## Signs of an infestation

- Extremely high numbers of small brown ants moving erratically
- Ants clustered in and around electrical boxes, A/C units, and outdoor outlets
- Populations that seem to explode overnight in mulch and under debris
- A noticeable drop in fire-ant mounds as crazy ants take over

## Why they are a problem

Beyond the overwhelming nuisance, tawny crazy ants cause equipment damage — shorting out electrical and irrigation controls — and their sheer density makes yards nearly unusable at peak season. Because they resist many standard baits, DIY efforts usually fail, letting the colony keep expanding.

## Preventing tawny crazy ants

- Remove leaf litter, mulch piles, landscape timbers, and debris that create harborage
- Keep vegetation and mulch back from the foundation and electrical equipment
- Fix moisture problems — leaks, irrigation overspray, and standing water
- Address honeydew-producing pests on ornamental plants
- Because they spread in potted plants and mulch, inspect anything brought onto the property
