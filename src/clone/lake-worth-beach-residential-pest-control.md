## Why Lake Worth Beach homes face year-round pest pressure

Lake Worth Beach packs historic wood-frame homes, a walkable downtown, a barrier-island beach, and a chain of freshwater lakes into a small footprint — a combination that keeps a distinctive mix of pests active all year.

Lake Worth Beach is one of Palm Beach County’s most historic and densely-built towns — a grid of 1920s bungalows and cottages, an artsy walkable downtown, a barrier-island beach with the landmark Casino, and the freshwater chain of lakes around John Prince Park to the west. In a humid subtropical climate with no real winter, pests here stay busy twelve months a year.

The town’s character is also its pest driver. Its aged wood-frame housing stock — among the oldest in the county — makes drywood termites and silverfish a headline concern, while the dense, walkable downtown and small lots let German cockroaches, ants, and rodents move easily between closely-spaced homes and businesses. On the water side, the Lake Worth Lagoon (the Intracoastal) and Lake Osborne and Lake Clarke to the west breed mosquitoes and no-see-ums and give roof rats seawall and shoreline routes into attics.

Protecting a Lake Worth Beach home well means respecting its age and density — favoring careful, targeted, no-tent treatments that protect historic structures while shutting down the pests that older, tightly-packed neighborhoods create. That’s how Bugsy’s approaches every home here.

## The Lake Worth Beach pest calendar: what to expect season by season

Pest pressure shifts through the year in South Florida. Here’s what tends to peak and when — so you stay ahead of it.

| Season | What peaks in Lake Worth Beach |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Termite swarm season — subterranean and drywood termites fly to start new colonies. The single most important time for a termite inspection. Ant and roach activity ramps up with the warmth. |
| Summer / Wet Season (Jun–Sep) | Peak mosquito and no-see-um pressure from daily rain and standing water. Palmetto bugs are highly active, rodents breed, and ant colonies hit full size — the hardest-hitting stretch of the year. |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | As the wet season winds down, rodents and roaches push indoors seeking shelter. Wildlife looks for attic harborage ahead of cooler nights. |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | South Florida’s mild winter means no true off-season — pests slow but never stop. Ideal window for exclusion work, attic sealing, and getting ahead of the spring surge. |
