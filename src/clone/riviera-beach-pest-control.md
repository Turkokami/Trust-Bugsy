## Why Riviera Beach stays under year-round pest pressure

Riviera Beach is defined by water — the Atlantic, the Lake Worth Lagoon, mangrove shorelines, and one of the busiest marina districts in the county. That saltwater edge is a powerhouse for salt-marsh mosquitoes and no-see-ums, which breed in the mangroves and tidal flats and fly inland for miles.

On land, the marinas, restaurants, and older mainland buildings give roaches and rodents constant food and harborage, while the barrier-island condos on Singer Island move pests through shared chases and trash rooms. Effective control here has to match the setting — waterfront home, condo, or commercial.

## The pests that define Riviera Beach

### No-See-Ums & Salt-Marsh Mosquitoes

Mangroves and tidal flats along the lagoon breed biting midges and salt-marsh mosquitoes by the thousands, especially after king tides and rain.

### Roaches

American and German roaches thrive around marinas, restaurants, and older buildings, pushing into homes and condos from drains and shared walls.

### Rodents

Roof rats travel the tree canopy, dock lines, and condo chases into attics and units; marinas and waterfront dining keep them fed.

### Ants, Termites & Iguanas

Ghost ants trail indoors, subterranean termites attack from moist soil, and iguanas colonize seawalls and canal banks.
