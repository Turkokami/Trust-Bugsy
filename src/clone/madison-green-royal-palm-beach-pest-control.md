## Why Madison Green sees steady pest activity

Madison Green is built around a golf course and a chain of lakes, with mature landscaping on nearly every street. It is a beautiful setting — and standing water plus dense greenery is exactly what mosquitoes, midges, and ants need to thrive. Homes backing the fairways, lakes, or water features feel it first and worst.

The mix of single-family homes and townhomes also means shared irrigation that keeps the soil moist all year, feeding multi-queen ant colonies and giving palmetto bugs and rodents easy harborage. A plan built around your specific street and lot keeps it in check.

## The pests Madison Green homes face

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

The golf-course lakes and water features are prime breeding grounds. Biting midges and mosquitoes swarm at dawn and dusk, hitting lakefront and fairway homes hardest.

### Ghost & White-Footed Ants

Moist, well-kept landscaping feeds enormous multi-queen ant colonies that trail indoors to kitchens and bathrooms in thin, hard-to-kill lines.

### Palmetto Bugs

American roaches move in from mulch beds, storm drains, and lake edges after rain, turning up in garages and lanais across the community.

### Roof Rats & Termites

Mature trees give roof rats a path to attics, while subterranean termites work up from the soil under slabs — both common in established RPB communities.
