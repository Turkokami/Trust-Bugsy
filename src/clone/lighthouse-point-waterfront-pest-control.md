## Why the Lighthouse Point waterfront homes need year-round coverage

Lighthouse Point is one of South Florida’s premier boating cities — nearly every home sits on a deep-water canal with a dock and seawall, minutes from the Hillsboro Inlet. That water is the city’s identity and its year-round pest engine: canals and mangroves breed no-see-ums and mosquitoes, and every seawall is iguana habitat.

Mature tropical landscaping gives roof rats a route into attics, and salt air keeps roaches probing for shelter. A plan built for canal-front living keeps the biting insects and wildlife in check.

## The pests the Lighthouse Point waterfront homes face

### No-See-Ums & Mosquitoes

The Intracoastal, canals, and mangrove edges breed biting midges and mosquitoes that swarm patios, docks, and pool decks at dawn and dusk.

### Iguanas & Wildlife

Seawalls and canal banks are prime iguana habitat — they burrow, damage landscaping, and foul docks and pools.

### Roof Rats

Mature tropical landscaping gives roof rats a route into attics, soffits, and outdoor kitchens.

### Palmetto Bugs & Termites

American roaches push up from drains after rain, and subterranean termites attack from the moist, sandy soil.
