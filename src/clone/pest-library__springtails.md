# Springtails

Pest Library

## Springtails

Subclass Collembola

Tiny specks that seem to jump like fleas when disturbed — harmless moisture-lovers that can appear in astonishing numbers around pools, bathrooms, potted plants, and damp new construction.

## How to identify them

Springtails are minuscule — a millimeter or two — and white, gray, or dark. The giveaway is movement: when disturbed they spring away suddenly using a forked, tail-like appendage (the furcula) tucked under the body, which is why they are often mistaken for fleas. Unlike fleas, they do not bite and are not associated with pets.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Springtails feed on mold, algae, fungi, and decaying organic matter and are utterly dependent on moisture. They thrive in damp soil, mulch, potted-plant soil, leaf litter, and around pools, and they flourish in the persistent humidity of South Florida.

They become noticeable when they mass in huge numbers — on pool decks and screens, in bathrooms and kitchens, around damp window frames, in the soil of overwatered houseplants, and especially in new construction where concrete and materials are still curing and damp. They are harmless but their sheer numbers alarm people.

## Signs of an infestation

- Clouds of tiny jumping specks around pools, drains, or damp soil
- Springtails in the soil of overwatered potted plants
- Masses on bathroom surfaces, window sills, or damp new concrete
- Numbers that spike in humid, wet conditions

## Why they are a problem

Springtails do not bite, sting, spread disease, or damage anything — they are purely a nuisance. The concern they raise is what their presence indicates: excess moisture, overwatering, or a damp problem area. Indoors in large numbers they are simply unpleasant and persistent until the moisture is corrected.

## How Bugsy’s treats springtails

Because springtails are a moisture symptom, control is really moisture management plus targeted treatment. Our technicians identify the damp source — overwatered plants, drainage, leaks, or humid harborage — treat the affected harborage, and apply materials where needed. Correcting the moisture is what eliminates them for good; treatment alone without drying the source is temporary.

## Preventing springtails

- Let potted-plant soil dry between waterings and avoid overwatering
- Fix leaks and reduce indoor humidity with ventilation and dehumidification
- Improve drainage and keep mulch and organic debris away from the foundation
- Seal gaps around windows, doors, and plumbing
- Address damp areas in new construction as materials cure
