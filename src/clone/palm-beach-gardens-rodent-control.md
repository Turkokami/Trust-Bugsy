## Why roof rats thrive in Palm Beach Gardens

The dominant rodent here is the roof rat — an agile climber that prefers to travel high, not on the ground. The seawalls of the Intracoastal and the golf-course lakes, dense landscaping, mature trees, and fences all lead straight to your roofline, soffits, and attic.

Once inside, they gnaw wiring (a fire risk), contaminate insulation, and reproduce fast — which is why speed and sealing matter.

## Signs you have rodents

If you’re noticing any of these, it’s time for an inspection.

### Noises in the attic or walls

Scratching, scurrying, or gnawing — especially at dusk and night — is the classic roof-rat giveaway.

### Droppings & grease marks

Dark droppings in the attic, garage, or pantry, and greasy smudge trails where rats travel.

### Gnaw marks & damage

Chewed wiring, insulation, boxes, and food packaging — roof rats gnaw constantly.

### Activity along the roofline

Rats seen running fence tops, tree limbs, or power lines toward the roof at dusk.

The roof rat is by far the most common rodent in Palm Beach Gardens — an agile climber that enters attics from the roofline via trees, fences, and utility lines. We also handle mice and, less commonly, Norway rats.
