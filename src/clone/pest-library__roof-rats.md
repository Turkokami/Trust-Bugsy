## How to Identify Roof Rats

Roof rats (Rattus rattus) are medium-sized rodents — about 13–18 inches including the tail — with sleek, dark brown or black fur, large ears, and a tail longer than their body. They’re much slimmer and more agile than Norway rats (which burrow). Roof rats are superb climbers and can run along power lines, climb rough walls, and leap several feet.

## Signs of Roof Rats in Your Home

- Scratching, scuffling, or running noises in the attic — particularly between sunset and midnight
- Small, dark, spindle-shaped droppings (about 1/2 inch) in the attic, on countertops, or along walls
- Gnaw marks on wood, plastic pipes, electrical wiring, or food packaging
- Grease smears (rub marks) along walls and pipes from their oily fur
- Nesting material — shredded insulation, paper, fabric — in attic corners
- Damaged or partially eaten fruit in trees or on the ground
- A strong, musky ammonia-like odor from a large infestation

## How Roof Rats Enter Your Home

Roof rats can squeeze through a hole the size of a quarter. Common entry points include:

- Gaps where roofing materials meet at the roofline
- Damaged or missing soffit vents
- The AC chase where air conditioning lines enter the home
- Plumbing vent pipes on the roof
- Gable vents with damaged screening
- Tree branches overhanging the roofline (their highway into your attic)
- Gaps around utility conduit and electrical boxes

## Health & Property Risks

Roof rats carry and transmit diseases including leptospirosis, salmonellosis, rat-bite fever, and murine typhus. They also carry fleas and mites that can spread additional diseases. Property risks include house fires from chewed wiring — a leading cause of unexplained residential fires in South Florida — and structural damage to insulation, ducts, and plumbing. Contaminated attic insulation must be removed and replaced to eliminate ongoing health risks after rodents are gone. ## Frequently Asked Questions

![Mouse on a kitchen floor near spilled food](https://trustbugsy.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Gemini_Generated_Image_ljelc4ljelc4ljel.webp)

### What’s the difference between roof rats and Norway rats?
Roof rats are slender, agile climbers with a tail longer than their body — almost always found in attics. Norway rats are larger, stockier, and burrow underground — more common in commercial properties and rarely found in residential attics in South Florida.

### Can I use snap traps and poison bait myself?
Snap traps can supplement professional trapping but aren’t sufficient alone for an established attic infestation. Poison bait without exclusion is a cycle — you’ll eliminate existing rats but new ones will enter through unsealed gaps indefinitely. Professional exclusion (sealing all entry points) is the critical step most DIY approaches skip.

### How many rats are typically in a South Florida attic?
Roof rat family groups range from 5 to 15 individuals. If you’re hearing scratching regularly, assume there’s an established family present — not just one or two individuals.

### Do I need to replace my attic insulation?
If rodents have been active in your attic for more than a few weeks, insulation replacement is strongly recommended. Rodent urine, droppings, and nesting material contaminate the insulation throughout, causing health risks and reducing the insulation’s thermal efficiency.
