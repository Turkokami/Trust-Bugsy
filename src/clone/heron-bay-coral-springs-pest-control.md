## Why Heron Bay homes need year-round coverage

Heron Bay sits at the northwest edge of Coral Springs, backing onto the Sawgrass corridor and the Everglades — a beautiful setting and a powerhouse for mosquitoes, no-see-ums, and wildlife. The community’s lakes and preserves breed biting insects in volume, and preserve edges bring iguanas, snakes, and the occasional larger visitor.

Fire ants work the turf, roof rats travel the canopy, and palmetto bugs push in after rain. Homes on the water or preserve edge need a plan built for that exposure.

## The pests Heron Bay homes face

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

Community lakes, canals, and the nearby Everglades edge breed mosquitoes and biting midges that swarm patios and pool decks at dawn and dusk.

### Fire Ants & Ghost Ants

Open turf and irrigated landscaping feed fire-ant mounds and ghost-ant colonies that trail indoors along counters and baseboards.

### Palmetto Bugs

American roaches push up from sewers and drains after rain, turning up in garages, lanais, and kitchens.

### Iguanas & Roof Rats

Iguanas colonize lake banks and canals, while roof rats travel the mature canopy into attics and soffits.
