## Why Palm Beach Gardens homes face year-round pest pressure

Palm Beach Gardens is a planned city of championship golf communities, man-made lakes, and preserved green space against the Loxahatchee Slough — a manicured, water-rich setting that keeps pests active all twelve months.

Palm Beach Gardens is a planned “garden city” famous for golf — home to PGA National and a string of championship gated communities — laid out around man-made lakes, lush landscaping, and preserved green space, with the Intracoastal on the east and the Loxahatchee Slough to the west. In a humid subtropical climate with no killing frost, pests here breed and forage year-round.

Both slab-built and wood-frame homes face termites from the soil and the structure.

Protecting a Palm Beach Gardens home means treating for a lush, lake-and-golf setting — lawn and ornamental pressure, lake-bred mosquitoes, and wildlife from the preserves — and tailoring the plan to your specific community. That’s how Bugsy’s treats every property here.

## The Palm Beach Gardens pest calendar: what to expect season by season

Pest pressure shifts through the year in South Florida. Here’s what tends to peak and when — so you stay ahead of it.

| Season | What peaks in Palm Beach Gardens |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Termite swarm season — subterranean and drywood termites fly to start new colonies. The single most important time for a termite inspection. Ant and roach activity ramps up with the warmth. |
| Summer / Wet Season (Jun–Sep) | Peak mosquito and no-see-um pressure from daily rain and standing water. Palmetto bugs are highly active, rodents breed, and ant colonies hit full size — the hardest-hitting stretch of the year. |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | As the wet season winds down, rodents and roaches push indoors seeking shelter. Wildlife looks for attic harborage ahead of cooler nights. |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | South Florida’s mild winter means no true off-season — pests slow but never stop. Ideal window for exclusion work, attic sealing, and getting ahead of the spring surge. |
