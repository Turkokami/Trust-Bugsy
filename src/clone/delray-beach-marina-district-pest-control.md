## Why the barrier island and marina drive coastal pests

The Marina and Beach District covers Delray east of the Intracoastal — the historic Marina District of bungalows and low-rise buildings lining the waterway, and the beachside condos, cottages, and estates off Ocean Boulevard and the public beach. It’s some of the most desirable real estate in the city, hemmed by water on two sides, and that coastal geography sets the pest agenda.

Seawalls, the marina, docks, and dense tropical landscaping give roof rats continuous elevated cover and a route to rooflines and condo upper floors. The Intracoastal and the ocean breeze deliver relentless **no-see-ums** and mosquitoes onto patios, balconies, and pool decks. And the district’s many condos and multi-unit buildings let German cockroaches and ants travel shared walls, chases, and trash rooms — so a single-unit fix rarely holds. Coastal living needs coastal-aware treatment.

## the Marina & Beach District pest control — questions we hear

### Do you service condos and multi-unit buildings in the Beach District?
Yes. Much of the district east of the Intracoastal is condos and multi-unit buildings, so we treat individual units and coordinate building-wide strategy for shared-wall pests like German cockroaches, and we provide documentation condo boards and managers need.

Those are almost certainly no-see-ums (biting midges) coming off the Intracoastal and ocean. They’re small enough to pass through normal screens, and standard mosquito-only service often misses them — we treat specifically for them as part of a coastal program.
