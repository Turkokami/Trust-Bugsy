## Why historic homes need historic-aware pest control

Del-Ida Park, platted in the 1920s with its distinctive diagonal streets, sits just northeast of the Atlantic-Swinton crossroads and anchors much of Delray’s historic district. Its Mediterranean Revival, Mission, and Florida-bungalow homes are architectural treasures — and they’re built from exactly what a certain set of pests want most: aged, dry structural wood.

That’s why **drywood termites** are the headline threat here. Unlike subterranean termites, drywood termites live entirely inside the framing, trim, and attic wood with no soil contact — and decades-old homes give them abundant harborage. Historic homes also tend to have the small gaps, older soffits, and settled foundations that let roof rats and ants in, plus the aged wood and paper that silverfish love. Treating a century-old home well means protecting its character — favoring targeted, no-tent methods over anything that risks the structure.

## The pests that threaten historic Delray homes

Aged wood, older construction, and settled foundations set the profile.

### Drywood Termites

The number-one risk for a historic home. They live inside dry framing and trim — look for pellet-like frass and discarded wings. We favor targeted no-tent BoraCare and spot treatment to protect the structure. See our drywood termite guide.

### Subterranean Termites

Older foundations and settling can open soil-contact paths for subterranean termites too. We inspect for both and treat each correctly.

### Roof Rats

Older soffits, vents, and roofline gaps on historic homes give roof rats easy entry. We trap and carefully seal without harming period details.

### Ants & Silverfish

Ghost and carpenter ants exploit older wood and moisture, while silverfish thrive on the aged paper, books, and framing common in these homes.

## Del-Ida Park pest control — questions we hear

### Why are drywood termites such a big deal in the historic district?
Del-Ida Park’s 1920s homes are built from aged structural wood, and drywood termites live entirely inside that framing and trim with no soil contact. Decades-old homes give them ideal, long-established harborage, which is why they’re the number-one termite threat here.

### Do I have to tent my historic home to treat drywood termites?
Usually not. We favor targeted no-tent BoraCare and spot treatments for drywood termites, so there’s no fumigation, no bagging food, and no leaving your home — a gentler approach that also avoids stressing an older structure. Extent of activity is confirmed at inspection.

### How do I know if I have termites in an old home?
Watch for tiny pellet-like droppings (frass) beneath wood, discarded wings near windows after a swarm, hollow-sounding or blistered wood, and small holes in trim. Our $99 inspection confirms whether it’s drywood, subterranean, or both.

### Will your treatment damage historic finishes or details?
No — we choose the least-invasive effective methods and work carefully around period detailing, both in treatment and in sealing rodent entry points. Protecting the home’s character is part of the job.

### Can you handle rats and silverfish in an older home too?
Yes. Older soffits and roofline gaps invite roof rats, and aged wood and paper draw silverfish — we address both alongside termite protection as part of a whole-home program for historic properties.
