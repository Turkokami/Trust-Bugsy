## Why Parrot Cove homes need year-round coverage

Parrot Cove is one of Lake Worth Beach’s prized historic neighborhoods, its 1920s homes shaded by grand old trees just off the Intracoastal. That canopy is a highway for roof rats, and decades-old wood is a target for drywood termites, while the lagoon breeds no-see-ums that drift into yards at dusk.

Settled construction develops the small gaps ants, roaches, and rodents use to get inside. We seal entry points and treat the source while respecting the character of a historic home.

## The pests Parrot Cove homes face

### Roof Rats & Rodents

Mature oaks and ficus put roof rats onto rooftops and into attics; settled bungalows offer plenty of gaps to exploit.

### Drywood & Subterranean Termites

Original wood framing and trim attract drywood termites, while subterranean termites attack from the soil.

### Ghost Ants

Older slabs and moist landscaping feed ghost-ant colonies that trail into kitchens and bathrooms.

### No-See-Ums & Palmetto Bugs

The Intracoastal and lagoon breed biting midges; American roaches push in from drains and mulch after rain.
