## Two roaches, two completely different problems

The large roaches most Jupiter homeowners call “palmetto bugs” are American cockroaches. They mostly live outdoors — in mulch, sewers, tree holes, and landscaping — and wander or fly indoors on warm, humid nights. Seeing them isn’t a sign of an unclean home; it’s a sign of our climate.

German cockroaches are the opposite — small, indoor-breeding roaches that hitchhike in on boxes and groceries and multiply fast in kitchens and bathrooms. In condos and multi-unit buildings they travel between units through shared walls and plumbing. They require baiting, not just spraying, to truly eliminate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I see big roaches even though my house is clean?
American cockroaches (palmetto bugs) live mostly outdoors in mulch, sewers, and landscaping and come inside on warm, humid nights. They reflect the climate, not cleanliness, and are controlled by treating exterior harborage and entry points.

### What’s the difference between palmetto bugs and German roaches?
Palmetto bugs (American roaches) are large and outdoor-living; German roaches are small and breed indoors in kitchens. They’re treated completely differently.

### Why won’t store-bought spray get rid of German roaches?
German roaches resist and avoid many sprays, and spraying can scatter them. Precision gel baiting that the roaches carry back to the harborage is what actually eliminates the population.
