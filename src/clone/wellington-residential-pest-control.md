## Why Wellington homes face year-round pest pressure

Wellington sits at the western edge of Palm Beach County against the wetlands and canals — an equestrian village of horse farms, man-made lakes, and gated family communities where standing water and open land keep pests active all twelve months.

Wellington is unlike the county’s coastal cities — a planned equestrian village on the western frontier against the Everglades, world-famous for the Winter Equestrian Festival, and built around horse farms, paddocks, man-made lakes, and gated golf and family communities. In a humid subtropical climate with no killing frost, and with water and open land everywhere, pests here breed and forage year-round.

Water and agriculture set the pest agenda. The C-51 and Lake Worth Drainage District canals, hundreds of community and equestrian lakes, and the wetlands and Loxahatchee refuge along the western edge create vast mosquito and no-see-um breeding habitat and push wildlife toward homes. Horse farms add their own pressure — feed, muck, and standing water draw flies, rodents, and fire ants, and barns and tack rooms invite roof rats. The village’s big family communities, meanwhile, pair broad St. Augustine lawns (chinch bugs, mole crickets, fire ants) with block homes that still face subterranean termites from the soil.

Effective pest control in Wellington means treating for a rural, water-rich, equestrian setting — not a coastal one — and tailoring the plan to whether you’re on a farm, a golf-course lot, or a family cul-de-sac. That’s how Bugsy’s treats every Wellington property.

## The Wellington pest calendar: what to expect season by season

Pest pressure shifts through the year in South Florida. Here’s what tends to peak and when — so you stay ahead of it.

| Season | What peaks in Wellington |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Termite swarm season — subterranean and drywood termites fly to start new colonies. The single most important time for a termite inspection. Ant and roach activity ramps up with the warmth. |
| Summer / Wet Season (Jun–Sep) | Peak mosquito and no-see-um pressure from daily rain and standing water. Palmetto bugs are highly active, rodents breed, and ant colonies hit full size — the hardest-hitting stretch of the year. |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | As the wet season winds down, rodents and roaches push indoors seeking shelter. Wildlife looks for attic harborage ahead of cooler nights. |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | South Florida’s mild winter means no true off-season — pests slow but never stop. Ideal window for exclusion work, attic sealing, and getting ahead of the spring surge. |

### Why do I keep seeing large roaches (“palmetto bugs”) even though my home is clean?
