## What Is No-Tent Termite Treatment?

No-tent treatment (also called localized or spot treatment) uses termiticide foam injected directly into the wood galleries where drywood termites are living. The foam flows through the termite colony’s tunnel system, reaching termites and eggs throughout the infested area. As termites come in contact with the product, they transfer it to nestmates — eventually reaching and eliminating the queen.

## Is No-Tent Right for My Home?

No-tent treatment is the right choice when:

- The drywood termite infestation is localized to specific areas (door frames, window sills, one section of the attic)
- Termite activity has been identified in fewer than 3-4 distinct areas of the home
- The infestation is in early to moderate stage
- You cannot vacate the property for a fumigation tent

If the infestation is severe, widespread, or affects the entire attic structure, we’ll recommend full tent fumigation instead. Our $99 inspection (applied to the cost of treatment) determines which option is appropriate.

## Termite tenting vs. no-tent treatment: which do you need?

Termite tenting (whole-structure fumigation) fills the entire home with gas, so it reaches drywood termites anywhere in the structure. No-tent treatment puts termiticide only into the infested wood. No-tent fits localized, early-to-moderate drywood activity; tenting is usually the answer when drywood termites are widespread or hidden in places that can’t be reached.

Bugsy’s specializes in no-tent treatment and does not perform fumigation. If our inspection shows your home needs tenting, we’ll tell you plainly and point you to a licensed fumigation company. Tenting is a drywood termite treatment — it does not treat [subterranean termites](https://trustbugsy.com/pest-library/subterranean-termites/), which live in the soil and need a soil or bait approach.

## How much does termite tenting cost?

Termite tenting is usually priced by the size of the structure, measured in cubic feet, plus the roof type and how hard the building is to seal. Other costs to ask about are the fumigant dose, nights away from home, bagging food and medicine, and repairs to roof tiles or landscaping once the tarps come off.

Ask any fumigator for a written quote that lists what’s included and what warranty comes with it. No-tent treatment is priced by how many areas need treatment, and our $99 inspection is applied to the cost of treatment.

## Termite fumigation alternatives

For drywood termites, the main alternatives to tenting are localized treatments that go straight to the infested wood:

- **Foam or liquid injection** into termite galleries — the core of our no-tent treatment.
- **Borate wood treatments** applied to exposed or attic wood to protect it from future attack.
- **Removing and replacing** small sections of heavily damaged wood such as trim, door frames or furniture.
- **Other localized methods**, such as heat, which some companies offer for specific areas.

None of these treat the whole structure at once, which is why an accurate inspection matters before you choose.

## How to prepare if you do tent

If your home does need fumigation, your fumigator will give you a checklist. It usually includes:

- Planning to stay elsewhere for the full fumigation period, typically a few days, with pets.
- Bagging or removing food, pet food, medicine and tobacco in the special bags the fumigator provides.
- Removing indoor plants and trimming outdoor plants back from the walls.
- Opening interior doors, cabinets and drawers so the gas can circulate.
- Following the fumigator’s instructions for gas service and pilot lights.
- Not going back inside until the fumigator has tested the air and cleared the home.

## Our No-Tent Treatment Process

### Step 1: $99 Inspection (applied to the cost of treatment)

Our licensed termite inspector performs a complete visual inspection of the interior and exterior, including the attic. We identify all areas of active drywood termite activity — frass (droppings), exit holes, and damaged wood. Per the [EPA termite guide](https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol/termites-how-identify-and-control-them), accurate identification of infestation scope is critical to selecting the right treatment.

### Step 2: Foam Injection

We drill small injection holes into infested wood and inject termiticide foam into the active termite galleries. The foam expands to fill the entire gallery system, ensuring product contacts all termites present.

### Step 3: Borate Dust Application

In the attic, we apply borate-based dust to wood surfaces — a long-lasting barrier that kills termites on contact and prevents re-infestation of treated areas.

### Step 4: One-Year Warranty

Your no-tent treatment is warranted for one year. If termite activity returns in treated areas within the warranty period, we retreat at no charge. Annual renewal inspections are available to maintain continuous coverage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if I need no-tent or full fumigation?
Our $99 inspection (applied to the cost of treatment) determines the right approach. Localized infestations in 1–3 areas are strong candidates for no-tent treatment. Widespread infestations throughout the attic or multiple rooms typically require fumigation.

### Is no-tent treatment as effective as fumigation?
For localized infestations, yes — no-tent treatment has a very high success rate when the infestation scope is accurately identified. It is not the right choice for severe, whole-home infestations.

### Does no-tent treatment smell?
The foam has minimal odor and dissipates quickly. There are no hazardous gases involved — no evacuation, no ventilation period required.

### Can I do anything to prevent termites from returning?
After treatment, we recommend annual inspections, addressing any moisture issues around the home, sealing exterior wood gaps, and considering a bait station program for subterranean termite prevention.
