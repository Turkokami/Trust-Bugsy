# Millipedes

Pest Library

## Millipedes

Class Diplopoda

The slow, cylindrical many-legged crawlers that coil into a spiral when disturbed — harmless decomposers that suddenly invade garages and homes by the dozens after heavy South Florida rains.

## How to identify them

Millipedes are slow, rounded, worm-like crawlers, brown to black, with many short legs — two pairs per body segment, which distinguishes them from the faster, flatter, one-pair-per-segment centipedes. When disturbed or dead they characteristically curl into a tight spiral.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Millipedes are beneficial decomposers, living in moist soil, mulch, and leaf litter where they feed on decaying plant matter. They are harmless — they do not bite — but when threatened many species secrete a defensive fluid that has an unpleasant odor and can stain surfaces or irritate skin slightly.

The problem is seasonal and dramatic: after heavy rains saturate the soil, or when populations peak, millipedes migrate in large numbers and pour into garages, lanais, and homes through the smallest ground-level gaps — often dozens at once, dead and alive, along the base of walls.

## Signs of an infestation

- Many slow, coiled or crawling millipedes along garage and foundation walls
- Sudden invasions right after heavy rain
- Dead millipedes accumulating at door thresholds and baseboards
- Activity concentrated in damp mulch and ground cover outside

## Why they are a problem

Millipedes cause no structural damage and do not bite, so they are a nuisance pest — but a memorable one, since they arrive in overwhelming numbers and their defensive secretions can stain and smell. Cleaning up repeated mass invasions is the main burden for homeowners.

## Preventing millipedes

- Keep mulch thin and pulled back from the foundation
- Improve drainage and correct irrigation overspray and low, wet spots
- Seal gaps under doors, garage thresholds, and along the foundation
- Remove leaf litter, grass clippings, and debris against the house
- Reduce exterior moisture and clutter where they gather before invading

### Quick facts

- Size: 1 to 2 inches
- Color: Brown to black
- Feature: Coils into a spiral; two leg pairs per segment
- Where: Mulch, soil, leaf litter
- Bite: No — may secrete odor fluid
- Peak activity: After heavy rain
