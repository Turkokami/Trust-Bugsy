## Why Do Rodents Target Attics Before a Storm?

Falling barometric pressure and rising winds push roof rats. Mice toward dry, elevated shelter long before a storm makes landfall. Attics offer exactly that: warm, quiet, and rarely disturbed. Once inside, these rodents burrow into soft materials using teeth built for constant gnawing, tearing apart insulation to build nesting sites. That destruction rarely stays contained to one corner of the attic.

In Palm Beach and Broward counties, the damage often compounds. Coastal humidity soaks insulation even before rodents arrive, and heat cycles common to South Florida weaken fibers further. A rodent looking for shelter finds insulation already primed for destruction — and nests without hesitation.

### What Materials Attract Rodents Into Attic Insulation?

Rodents nest in almost any organic material available to them, including cotton, wood, paper, and insulation fibers. Given the choice between exposed insulation and sealed storage, rats and mice choose the path of least resistance every time.

## How Can You Rodent-Proof Your Attic Before Landfall?

Two habits determine whether an attic survives storm season intact: what’s stored inside it, and what it’s insulated with. Rodents seeking shelter from wind and rain gravitate toward soft, chewable material, and an unprotected attic offers exactly that. Homes across hurricane-prone regions face this risk every year as storms push wildlife indoors.

Storage matters more than most homeowners assume. Cardboard boxes and loosely sealed plastic bags give rodents easy entry points, since their teeth tear through both with little effort. Airtight plastic containers remove that vulnerability entirely, sealing off nesting material rodents would otherwise shred and repurpose.

### Does old insulation attract rodents?

Yes. The condition of attic insulation directly affects a home’s exposure to infestation. Worn or compressed material offers weaker resistance to burrowing pests. Homes with degraded insulation face a higher risk of rodents settling in before, during, and after a storm.
