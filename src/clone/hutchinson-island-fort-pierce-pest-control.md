## Why Hutchinson Island homes need year-round coverage

Hutchinson Island is a barrier island with the Atlantic on one side and the Indian River Lagoon on the other, ringed by mangroves and dunes. That means no-see-ums and salt-marsh mosquitoes from both directions — the island gets some of the heaviest biting-insect pressure on the Treasure Coast.

Seawalls draw iguanas, condo chases and landscaping give roof rats routes indoors, and salt air keeps roaches probing for shelter. A plan built for barrier-island exposure keeps it in check.

## The pests Hutchinson Island homes face

### No-See-Ums & Sandflies

The Indian River Lagoon, mangroves, and tidal flats breed biting midges in notorious numbers — the Treasure Coast’s signature pest, slipping through screens at dawn and dusk.

### Salt-Marsh Mosquitoes

Lagoon marshes and standing water breed aggressive salt-marsh mosquitoes that fly inland for miles, worst after rain and king tides.

### Iguanas & Roof Rats

Seawalls and canal banks draw iguanas, while mature landscaping gives roof rats a route into attics and soffits.

### Palmetto Bugs & Termites

American roaches push up from drains after rain, and subterranean termites attack from the warm, sandy soil.
