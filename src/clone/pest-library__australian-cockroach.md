# Australian Cockroach

Pest Library

## Australian Cockroach

Periplaneta australasiae

A large tropical roach easily told apart by the pale yellow band across its shoulders and yellow streaks at the base of its wings — thoroughly at home in South Florida’s gardens, greenhouses, and mulch.

## How to identify them

The Australian cockroach looks much like the American roach but is a bit smaller and carries two clear field marks: a pale yellow band along the rear edge of the shield behind the head (pronotum), and yellow streaks along the front edges of the wings near the shoulders. Adults are reddish-brown and about an inch to an inch and a quarter.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

This is a tropical, peridomestic species that thrives in Florida’s climate. It lives outdoors in mulch, leaf litter, wood piles, tree holes, and around foundations, and it is a common greenhouse and garden pest — unusually for a roach, it will feed on living plants, chewing soft new growth and seedlings.

Like other large roaches it seeks humidity and warmth and moves indoors through gaps, especially in the wet season, and it flies to lights.

## Signs of an infestation

- Large reddish roaches with a yellow shoulder band on patios and in garages
- Chewing damage to seedlings and tender plants in the landscape
- Activity in mulch, greenhouses, and around outdoor water sources
- Roaches entering through door gaps, vents, and around utility lines

## Why they are a problem

Australian roaches are both a garden pest — damaging plants — and a home-invading nuisance that can track bacteria from decaying matter onto surfaces indoors. Their droppings and shed skins add to household allergens, and their size and flight make them alarming when they get inside.

## How Bugsy’s treats Australian cockroaches

Control mirrors other peridomestic roaches: reduce and treat outdoor harborage in mulch, plant beds, and enclosures; apply targeted materials along foundations and entry routes; and seal the gaps that let them indoors. We factor in the landscape and moisture conditions that support them so the treatment holds through the wet season.

## Preventing Australian cockroaches

- Keep mulch thin and away from the foundation and clear plant debris
- Water plants in the morning so beds are not damp overnight
- Trim vegetation off the structure and screen vents
- Use yellow exterior lighting and keep doors and windows sealed
- Seal cracks and utility penetrations around the home
