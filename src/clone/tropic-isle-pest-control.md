## Why waterfront living means different pests

Tropic Isle is a planned finger-canal community off US-1 near the south city limit, where nearly every home has a seawall, a dock, and canal access that runs to the Intracoastal and out to the ocean. It’s prized for exactly that — and that water-everywhere layout reshapes which pests you fight and how.

Seawalls, docks, and dense waterfront landscaping give roof rats continuous cover and elevated travel routes right to the house, with no need to cross open ground. The canals and the Intracoastal breed mosquitoes and, especially, **no-see-ums** (biting midges) that come off the water at dawn and dusk and slip through standard screens onto docks and patios. Boats, dock boxes, and waterfront storage add their own ant and pest issues. Standard inland pest routes miss what a Tropic Isle property actually needs.

Tropic Isle’s seawalls, docks, and waterfront landscaping give roof rats continuous elevated cover right up to the house, so canal-front homes see heavy pressure. The fix is trapping plus sealing the waterside roofline and soffit gaps they enter through.
