# Little Black Ants

Pest Library

## Little Black Ants

Monomorium minimum

A shiny, jet-black ant so small it is easy to miss until the trails appear — a very common South Florida invader that nests in woodwork, masonry, and soil and feeds on almost anything in your kitchen.

## How to identify them

Little black ants live up to the name: a uniform, shiny black and only about a sixteenth of an inch long. Individually they are easy to overlook, so infestations are usually spotted as thin dark trails moving along counters, baseboards, and foundation cracks.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Colonies have multiple queens and can be large. They nest in an unusually wide range of spots — decaying wood, wall voids, masonry joints, under mulch and stones, and in open soil — which is why they turn up both indoors and out.

They are true generalists, feeding on sweets, greasy foods, proteins, and honeydew from garden pests. That flexibility, plus their small size and ability to exploit tiny cracks, makes them one of the most frequently encountered household ants in the region.

## Signs of an infestation

- Thin trails of very small black ants along walls and counters
- Ants emerging from cracks in masonry, foundations, and woodwork
- Small piles of soil (like fine sand) near nest openings in paving or brick
- Activity in kitchens and pantries around a range of foods

## Why they are a problem

Little black ants are primarily a contamination nuisance — they get into stored food and forage across kitchen surfaces. Their weak sting is rarely noticeable. The frustration is persistence: with multiple queens and flexible nesting, they recolonize quickly if only the visible trail is treated.

## Preventing little black ants

- Seal cracks in foundations, masonry, and around windows and doors
- Keep mulch, stones, and wood debris away from the foundation
- Wipe up crumbs and spills and store food in sealed containers
- Fix moisture problems that attract and sustain colonies
- Trim plants touching the house that give ants a bridge indoors

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/16 inch (about 1.5 mm)
- Color: Shiny black
- Bite or sting: Weak sting, rarely felt
- Nests: Woodwork, masonry, soil, voids
- Diet: Sweets, grease, proteins
- Peak activity: Summer months
