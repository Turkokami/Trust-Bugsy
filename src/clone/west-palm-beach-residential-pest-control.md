## Why West Palm Beach homes face year-round pest pressure

West Palm Beach spans historic waterfront districts, a dense downtown, and sprawling western neighborhoods against the Grassy Waters wetlands — a big, varied city where pests stay active all twelve months.

West Palm Beach is the county’s largest and most urban city — a downtown of high-rises and restaurants along the Intracoastal, historic waterfront neighborhoods like El Cid and Flamingo Park, and miles of family neighborhoods stretching west toward Grassy Waters Preserve and the wetlands. In a humid subtropical climate with no killing frost, pests here breed and forage year-round.

The city’s range is its pest story. Along Flagler Drive and the Intracoastal (the Lake Worth Lagoon), waterfront condos and homes face mosquitoes, no-see-ums, and roof rats traveling seawalls and docks. Historic districts like El Cid, Flamingo Park, and Old Northwood — full of 1920s Mediterranean and wood-frame homes — are prime drywood-termite country. Downtown around Clematis Street and Rosemary Square, dense housing and a busy dining scene push German cockroaches and rodents into nearby blocks. And western West Palm, near Grassy Waters and the canals, puts family neighborhoods against major mosquito and wildlife habitat.

Protecting a West Palm Beach home means matching the plan to the district — waterfront, historic core, downtown, or western wetland edge — and to the pests each specific setting creates. That’s how Bugsy’s treats every property in the city.

## The West Palm Beach pest calendar: what to expect season by season

Pest pressure shifts through the year in South Florida. Here’s what tends to peak and when — so you stay ahead of it.

| Season | What peaks in West Palm Beach |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Termite swarm season — subterranean and drywood termites fly to start new colonies. The single most important time for a termite inspection. Ant and roach activity ramps up with the warmth. |
| Summer / Wet Season (Jun–Sep) | Peak mosquito and no-see-um pressure from daily rain and standing water. Palmetto bugs are highly active, rodents breed, and ant colonies hit full size — the hardest-hitting stretch of the year. |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | As the wet season winds down, rodents and roaches push indoors seeking shelter. Wildlife looks for attic harborage ahead of cooler nights. |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | South Florida’s mild winter means no true off-season — pests slow but never stop. Ideal window for exclusion work, attic sealing, and getting ahead of the spring surge. |

### Why do I keep seeing large roaches (“palmetto bugs”) even though my home is clean?
American cockroaches (palmetto bugs) largely live outdoors — in mulch, sewers, and landscaping — and wander or fly indoors on warm, humid nights. They’re a sign of our climate, not an unclean home, and are best controlled by treating exterior harborage and entry points.
