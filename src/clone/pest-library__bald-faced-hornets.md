# Bald-Faced Hornets

Pest Library

## Bald-Faced Hornets

Dolichovespula maculata

The large black-and-white wasps behind those big gray football-shaped nests hanging in trees and on structures — highly protective of the nest and able to sting again and again.

## How to identify them

Despite the name, bald-faced hornets are a type of yellow jacket, not a true hornet. They are larger than typical yellow jackets, black with a distinctive white (“bald”) face and white markings. The signature is the nest: a large, gray, papery, football- or teardrop-shaped structure with a single opening near the bottom, hanging in a tree, shrub, or on a building.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

A queen founds the nest in spring, and the colony grows through summer to a few hundred workers inside the enclosed paper envelope. They hunt insects — including other wasps and flies — making them beneficial, but they are intensely protective of the nest.

Bald-faced hornets will aggressively defend a wide zone around the nest, pursue a perceived threat, and sting repeatedly. Because nests are often high or hidden in foliage, people frequently do not notice one until they are already too close, such as while trimming a hedge or tree.

## Signs of an infestation

- A large gray, football-shaped paper nest in a tree, shrub, or on the eaves
- Hornets streaming from a single opening near the bottom of the nest
- Aggressive wasps patrolling an area of the yard
- Nests discovered while pruning, mowing, or working near the roofline

## Why they are a problem

Bald-faced hornets are among the most defensive stinging insects, and their large colonies mean many wasps ready to respond at once. A nest near a walkway, play area, or work zone is a serious hazard, particularly for anyone allergic, and their willingness to pursue and sting repeatedly makes DIY removal dangerous.

## Preventing bald-faced hornets

- Inspect trees, shrubs, and eaves early in the season for small starter nests
- Keep an eye on dense foliage near walkways and play areas
- Do not prune or work near a nest until it has been professionally handled
- Manage the insects around the yard that hornets hunt
- Call a professional for any established aerial nest rather than approaching it
