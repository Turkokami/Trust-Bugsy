## Why Delray Beach is a mosquito hot spot

Mosquitoes need only a bottle-cap of standing water to breed — and Delray Beach is surrounded by it. The Intracoastal and the finger canals of Tropic Isle and the Marina District, the lakes around Lake Ida, and the wetland preserves out toward Wakodahatchee all create prime breeding habitat, and the daily rain of our May-to-October wet season refills every low spot, planter, and gutter in between.

On top of mosquitoes, coastal Delray deals with **no-see-ums** (biting midges) — tiny, vicious biters that come off the water and slip through standard screens, especially near the Intracoastal at dawn and dusk. Left untreated, the combination can make a beautiful yard, patio, or dock genuinely unusable for months.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often do you treat for mosquitoes?

Through Delray’s wet season, a monthly recurring barrier treatment is typical — it keeps the population knocked down as rain constantly refills breeding sites. We’ll recommend the right cadence for your property.

### Are mosquito treatments safe for my pets and pollinators?

We apply with pet and pollinator safety in mind — targeting mosquito resting areas and breeding water, timing applications appropriately, and avoiding blooming plants where practical. Your technician will explain any brief precautions.

### Can you do anything about no-see-ums?

Yes. Coastal Delray Beach gets significant no-see-um (biting midge) pressure off the water, and we include treatment targeted at them as part of our program.

### Do I need to be home for the treatment?

No — most mosquito service is performed on the exterior, so you don’t need to be home. We’ll let you know when we’ve been out.

### Will one treatment get rid of my mosquitoes?

A single treatment provides relief, but in Delray’s climate mosquitoes are constantly re-breeding, so recurring service through the wet season is what keeps your yard usable all summer.

### What can I do to help between treatments?

Dump standing water weekly — planters, saucers, bird baths, tarps, and clogged gutters — and keep bromeliads flushed. Removing breeding water is the single most effective thing a homeowner can do.
