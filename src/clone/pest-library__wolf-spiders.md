## Wolf Spiders

Family Lycosidae

Large, fast, hairy ground hunters that chase down prey instead of spinning webs — intimidating to find sprinting across a floor, but not dangerous, and often a sign there are other insects to hunt.

## How to identify them

Wolf spiders are large, robust, hairy spiders in mottled brown and gray that blend into soil, mulch, and flooring. They do not build webs to catch prey — instead they hunt on the ground, running down insects with speed. Their size, speed, and habit of appearing suddenly at floor level make them one of the most alarming spiders to encounter, though they are not aggressive.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Wolf spiders live at ground level — in mulch, leaf litter, grass, and along foundations — and are solitary nocturnal hunters. Females are devoted parents, carrying their egg sac attached to the spinnerets and then carrying the newly hatched spiderlings on their backs, which is a distinctive sight.

They wander indoors chasing prey or seeking shelter, especially as weather shifts, turning up in garages, along baseboards, and in ground-floor rooms. They bite only defensively if trapped against skin or handled, and their bite — while it can be painful — is not medically significant to most people.

## Signs of an infestation

- Large, fast spiders running across floors at ground level rather than in webs
- A female carrying an egg sac or spiderlings on her back
- Sightings in garages, along baseboards, and in ground-floor rooms
- Presence of other insects — their prey — around the home

## Why they are a problem

Wolf spiders are beneficial hunters and pose little medical risk, so the concern is mainly their intimidating appearance and the startle of a large spider indoors. Frequent sightings usually mean there is a healthy supply of other insects to hunt and easy ground-level access, both worth addressing.

## How Bugsy’s treats wolf spiders

Since wolf spiders follow prey and enter at ground level, control targets both: our technicians reduce the insect populations they hunt, treat harborage in mulch and along the foundation, apply a targeted perimeter, and seal ground-level entry points. Cutting off the food and the access is what keeps them from wandering in.

## Preventing wolf spiders

- Seal gaps under doors, garage thresholds, and along the foundation
- Reduce exterior insects with yellow lighting and perimeter treatment
- Keep mulch, leaf litter, and ground cover back from the house
- Reduce ground-level clutter in garages and storage areas
- Trim grass and vegetation along foundation lines where they hunt

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/2 to over 1 inch body
- Color: Brown, gray, mottled — camouflaged
- Web: None — active hunter
- Where: Ground level, garages, low walls
- Bite: Defensive only; not medically significant
- Peak activity: Warm months
