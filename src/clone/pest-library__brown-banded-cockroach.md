# Brown-Banded Cockroach

Pest Library

## Brown-Banded Cockroach

Supella longipalpa

A small indoor roach that breaks the usual rules — instead of hiding under damp sinks, it prefers warm, dry, high spots like upper cabinets, electronics, and behind wall art, and it spreads easily in furniture.

## How to identify them

Brown-banded roaches are small — about half an inch — and light brown with two lighter, yellowish bands running across the wings and abdomen, most visible in nymphs. They are often confused with German cockroaches but are lighter and behave very differently.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Where German roaches crowd around kitchen and bathroom moisture, brown-banded roaches seek warm, dry, elevated harborage: the tops of upper cabinets, behind picture frames and wall clocks, inside electronics and appliances, in closets, and behind trim near the ceiling. This spread-out, high-up habit makes them harder to find.

They breed indoors year-round, and females glue their egg cases to furniture, cardboard, and the undersides of shelves — which is why brown-banded infestations frequently arrive with used furniture, appliances, or boxes.

## Signs of an infestation

- Small light-brown roaches high on walls, in cabinets, or inside electronics
- Egg cases glued under shelves, furniture, and drawers
- Roaches scattered throughout the home rather than clustered at sinks
- A new problem appearing after bringing in used furniture or boxes

## Why they are a problem

Like other roaches, brown-banded roaches contaminate food and surfaces and contribute to indoor allergens that can trigger asthma. Their preference for spreading throughout a home — including bedrooms and living areas — and hiding in hard-to-reach elevated spots makes DIY control especially frustrating.

## Preventing brown-banded cockroaches

- Inspect used furniture, electronics, and boxes before bringing them inside
- Reduce clutter — especially cardboard — that offers harborage
- Vacuum cabinet tops, closets, and behind wall-hung items
- Keep food sealed and clean up crumbs throughout the home, not just the kitchen
- Call early — scattered infestations grow harder to control over time
