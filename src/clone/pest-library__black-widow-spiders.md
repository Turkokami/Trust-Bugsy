## How to identify them

The mature female southern black widow is glossy jet-black with a distinctive red hourglass marking on the underside of the abdomen — the classic warning sign. She is about half an inch in body length. Her web is an irregular, tangled snare, notably strong and often built low in a dark, protected corner. Males are much smaller and not a concern.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Black widows favor dark, dry, undisturbed places: sheds, garages, crawlspaces, woodpiles, utility and meter boxes, under outdoor furniture and grills, and in clutter along foundations. They are reclusive and not aggressive — a widow will retreat rather than confront, and bites almost always happen when the spider is unknowingly pressed against skin, such as reaching into a box or pulling on a stored item.

The female’s venom is a neurotoxin, and a bite can cause significant pain, muscle cramping, and systemic symptoms that warrant prompt medical attention, though fatalities are very rare with modern care.

## Signs of an infestation

- Glossy black spiders with a red hourglass in sheltered, low corners
- Strong, irregular, tangled webs in garages, sheds, and meter boxes
- Round, papery egg sacs in the web
- Activity in stored, cluttered, or seldom-disturbed areas

## Why they are a problem

Black widows are one of the few Florida spiders whose bite is a genuine medical concern, particularly for children, older adults, and anyone with health conditions. Because they hide in exactly the spots where people reach without looking — storage, woodpiles, utility boxes — an accidental bite can still happen even though the spider itself avoids people.

## Preventing black widow spiders

- Wear gloves when reaching into stored boxes, woodpiles, and utility spaces
- Reduce clutter in garages, sheds, and along the foundation
- Knock down webs and remove egg sacs as they appear
- Seal gaps and screen vents on sheds, garages, and crawlspaces
- Manage the insects that spiders feed on around lighting and the perimeter
