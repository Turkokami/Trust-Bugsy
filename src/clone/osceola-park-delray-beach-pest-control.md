## Why Osceola Park homes need year-round coverage

Osceola Park is one of Delray’s older neighborhoods, a walkable grid of established homes just south of Atlantic Avenue and the downtown. Mature trees line the streets — a highway for roof rats — and decades-old wood is a target for termites.

Settled construction develops the gaps ants, roaches, and rodents use to get inside, and downtown-edge density keeps them moving between properties. We seal entry points and treat the source, not just the trail.

## The pests Osceola Park homes face

### Roof Rats & Rodents

Mature trees put roof rats onto rooftops and into attics; settled homes offer plenty of gaps to exploit.

### Drywood & Subterranean Termites

Older wood framing attracts drywood termites, while subterranean termites attack from the soil.

### Ghost Ants

Older slabs and moist landscaping feed ghost-ant colonies that trail into kitchens and baths.

### Palmetto Bugs

American roaches push in from drains, mulch, and downtown-area moisture after rain.
