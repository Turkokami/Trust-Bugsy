# Oriental Cockroach

Pest Library

## Oriental Cockroach

Blatta orientalis

The dark, shiny roach many people call a “water bug” — slow-moving, tied to damp drains and decaying matter, and known for a strong, musty odor when it is around in numbers.

## How to identify them

Oriental cockroaches are about an inch long and a very dark, almost black, glossy color. Males have short wings and females essentially none, and neither flies. They move slowly compared with other roaches, and a population often announces itself with a distinctive musty smell.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Oriental roaches are strongly tied to moisture and cooler, damp spaces. They shelter in drains, sewer lines, water-meter boxes, crawlspaces, under mulch and stones, in leaf litter, and around foundations, feeding on decaying organic matter and garbage.

They typically enter buildings at ground level — under doors, through drains, and along utility lines — especially when outdoor conditions dry out or flood. They are considered among the filthier roaches because of the environments they frequent.

## Signs of an infestation

- Dark, glossy, slow-moving roaches near drains, garages, and foundations
- A strong musty odor in affected areas
- Activity in damp crawlspaces, around water meters, and under mulch
- Roaches coming up through floor drains or appearing after rain

## Why they are a problem

Because oriental roaches travel through drains, sewers, and decaying matter, they can carry and spread bacteria onto surfaces, making them a notable sanitation and health concern. Their droppings and shed skins add to indoor allergens, and their musty odor is unpleasant on its own.

## Preventing oriental cockroaches

- Fix leaks, improve drainage, and eliminate standing water around the foundation
- Keep mulch, leaf litter, and debris away from the structure
- Seal gaps under doors and around pipes and utility penetrations
- Keep floor drains maintained and covered
- Store garbage in sealed containers away from the house
