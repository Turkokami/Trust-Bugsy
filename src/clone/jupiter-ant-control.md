## Why ants are the #1 call in Jupiter

South Florida’s warmth and humidity make it ant paradise, and Jupiter homes see several species — often at once. The most common are tiny tropical ants (ghost and white-footed ants) that trail across kitchens and baths by the thousands, plus fire ants in the yard and occasional carpenter ants in damp wood.

Here’s the catch: several of these species “bud” — spray them with the wrong product and the colony splits into several new ones. Correct identification and the right baiting strategy are what separate lasting control from a problem that keeps coming back.

## The ants we treat in Jupiter

Each is identified and treated differently.

### Ghost & White-Footed Ants

Tiny pale ants that trail in huge numbers across counters and baths. They bud when sprayed — we bait, not blast.

### Big-Headed Ants

Trail along baseboards and patios and push up soil piles; managed with targeted baiting.

### Fire Ants

Aggressive stinging ants that colonize sunny turf; treated at the mound and across the yard.

### Carpenter & Other Ants

Larger ants tied to moisture and wood; we find the nest and the conducive conditions feeding it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I keep getting ants even after spraying?
Because several South Florida ant species bud — spraying splits the colony into new ones. Lasting control means identifying the species and baiting it back to the nest, which is exactly how we treat.

### What are the tiny ants trailing in my kitchen?
Almost certainly ghost or white-footed ants — two of the most common household ants here. They’re harmless but relentless, and they respond to baiting, not spraying.

### Do you treat fire ants in the yard?
Yes. We treat fire ant mounds and broadcast-treat the yard to keep the sunny turf clear, which matters for families and pets.

### Are ant treatments safe for kids and pets?
Yes. We use targeted baits and low-impact products placed where they reach the ants but not your family, with any brief precautions explained.

### How fast will the ants be gone?
You’ll usually see a sharp drop within days as the bait reaches the colony, with full control over a couple of weeks as the nest collapses.
