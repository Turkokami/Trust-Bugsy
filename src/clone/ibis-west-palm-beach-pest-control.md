## Why Ibis sees some of the heaviest pressure in West Palm Beach

Ibis wraps luxury homes around three golf courses and a chain of lakes, and its western edge backs directly onto the Grassy Waters Preserve — a protected Everglades wetland. That is a spectacular setting and a relentless source of mosquitoes, biting midges, and wildlife pushing toward the homes.

The preserve and lakes mean heavier-than-normal mosquito and no-see-um activity, plus fire ants in the turf and roof rats moving through the mature landscaping. Homes on the water or preserve edge need a plan built for that exposure, not a standard tract-home routine.

## The pests Ibis homes face

### Mosquitoes & No-See-Ums

The lakes and the adjacent Grassy Waters wetland breed biting insects in volume; preserve- and lake-edge homes get swarmed at dawn and dusk.

### Fire Ants & Yard Ants

Manicured turf and fairway edges are ideal for fire-ant mounds and nuisance ants that push toward the home.

### Rodents & Wildlife

Roof rats travel the mature canopy into attics, and the preserve brings iguanas, snakes, and the occasional raccoon to canal banks and yards.

### Palmetto Bugs & Termites

American roaches move in from mulch and lake edges after rain, while subterranean termites attack from the moist soil.
