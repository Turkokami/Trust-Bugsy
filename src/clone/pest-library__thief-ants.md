# Thief Ants

Pest Library

## Thief Ants

Solenopsis molesta

Among the tiniest household ants in Florida, thief ants earn their name by nesting next to other ant colonies and stealing their brood and food — and their love of grease sets them apart from the sweet-seeking ants they are often confused with.

## How to identify them

Thief ants are so small they are sometimes called “grease ants” and are easily mistaken for pharaoh ants. They are pale yellow to light brown, with a two-segmented waist and, under magnification, a two-segmented antennal club. Their minute size lets them exploit cracks other ants cannot.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

The name comes from their habit of nesting close to — even inside the walls of — larger ant colonies, then slipping in to steal food and larvae. This behavior keeps them hidden and hard to trace.

Unlike many household ants that chase sweets, thief ants strongly prefer greasy and protein-rich foods — meats, cheeses, and oils — which matters for baiting: sweet baits alone often fail. They nest in tiny wall and floor voids, cabinet cracks, and outdoors under objects and in soil.

## Signs of an infestation

- Extremely tiny yellowish ants around greasy foods and pantries
- Trails so faint they are easy to miss until food is disturbed
- Activity in cabinets, around meat and oil storage, and near other ant nests
- Frustration with sweet ant baits that the ants ignore

## Why they are a problem

Thief ants contaminate stored food, particularly protein and greasy items, and their tiny size lets them infiltrate packaging and cabinetry that larger ants cannot reach. Because they are easily misidentified, they are often treated with the wrong bait, prolonging the infestation.

## Preventing thief ants

- Store greasy and protein foods — and pet food — in sealed containers
- Clean grease from stovetops, range hoods, and cabinet surfaces
- Seal cracks in cabinets, floors, and walls
- Keep the exterior perimeter clear of debris and objects on soil
- Address any co-occurring ant colonies they may be nesting beside

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/32 to 1/16 inch (0.5-1.5 mm)
- Color: Pale yellow to light brown
- Bite or sting: Tiny sting, rarely felt
- Nests: Tiny cracks, voids, near other ants
- Diet: Grease, protein, seeds
- Peak activity: Warm months
