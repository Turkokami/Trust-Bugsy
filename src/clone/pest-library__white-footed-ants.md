# White-Footed Ants

Pest Library

## White-Footed Ants

Technomyrmex difficilis

One of South Florida’s most abundant nuisance ants — black-bodied with pale, almost translucent feet, they build colonies that can number in the millions and stream along fence lines, tree trunks, and into kitchens in unbroken trails.

## How to identify them

White-footed ants are small — roughly an eighth of an inch — with a dark brownish-black body and distinctly paler, yellowish-white feet (tarsi) that give the species its name. They have a single node in the waist and no stinger.

They are most often noticed as dense, persistent trails running up exterior walls, along soffits and gutters, over tree limbs, and through weep holes into the home. Where many nuisance ants form thin trails, white-footed ant trails are frequently thick and continuous.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Colonies are enormous and contain many reproductive females, which is the key reason they are so hard to knock down: instead of one queen, a single infestation may draw from thousands of egg-layers spread across satellite nests in wall voids, attics, mulch beds, palm boots, and tree canopies.

They are dedicated honeydew feeders, farming aphids, scale, and mealybugs on ornamental plants for the sugary secretions those insects produce. This outdoor sugar supply is why baiting alone often underperforms — the colony simply is not hungry enough to take much bait.

White-footed ants do not sting, do not bite in any meaningful way, and do not damage wood or wiring. The problem they create is sheer numbers and relentless foraging indoors.

## Signs of an infestation

- Broad, continuous ant trails on exterior walls, fences, and tree trunks
- Ants emerging from weep holes, soffits, and around window frames
- Sudden indoor trails to kitchens, pet bowls, and bathrooms
- Heavy aphid or scale activity on landscape plants near the house

## Why they are a problem

The nuisance is the point: a mature colony can put so many foragers into a home that counters, pantries, and pet-feeding areas are overrun within days. Because they contaminate exposed food and are nearly impossible to ignore, they are one of the top ant complaints Bugsy’s hears from Palm Beach and Broward homeowners.

## How Bugsy’s treats white-footed ants

Effective control targets the colony where it lives rather than the trail you see. Our technicians trace foraging lines back to nesting sites, treat wall voids and satellite nests directly, and apply a non-repellent exterior perimeter product that foragers carry back and share — reaching the reproductives that baiting alone can miss.

Because these ants feed on plant honeydew, we also address the ornamental plants fueling the colony and seal the entry points — weep holes, utility penetrations, and soffit gaps — that let trails move indoors.

## Preventing white-footed ants

- Trim trees and shrubs so branches do not touch the roof or walls — these are ant highways
- Keep mulch pulled a few inches back from the foundation
- Control aphids and scale on landscape plants to cut off their food
- Seal weep holes with screening and caulk gaps around windows, pipes, and soffits
- Keep kitchens wiped down and store sweet foods in sealed containers

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/8 inch (about 3 mm)
- Color: Black body, pale/yellow feet
- Bite or sting: No
- Structural damage: No
- Colony size: Tens of thousands to millions
- Peak activity: Spring through fall
