## Why Jupiter homes face year-round pest pressure

Jupiter blends Atlantic coast, the Loxahatchee River, and rural western farms into one town — and that mix of salt water, fresh water, and open country keeps pests active all twelve months.

Jupiter sits at the north end of Palm Beach County where the Loxahatchee River meets the Atlantic at the historic Jupiter Inlet and lighthouse. It ranges from beachfront and deep-water waterfront communities, through the walkable town center of Abacoa, out to the large rural homesteads of Jupiter Farms against the western wetlands. In a humid subtropical climate with no killing frost, pests here breed and forage year-round.

Water — both kinds — drives the pressure. The Loxahatchee River, the inlet, the Intracoastal, and the canals of Admirals Cove and the waterfront communities create prime mosquito and no-see-um habitat and give roof rats seawall and dock routes into homes. To the west, Jupiter Farms’ acreage, canals, and wetland edges add wildlife, rodents around barns and outbuildings, and heavy mosquito breeding. The town’s mix of older coastal homes and newer inland communities means both drywood and subterranean termites are in play.

Effective pest control in Jupiter means matching the plan to the setting — barrier-island beach home, deep-water estate, Abacoa townhome, or Jupiter Farms homestead — and to the pests each creates. That’s how Bugsy’s treats every Jupiter property.

## The Jupiter neighborhoods we protect

Every part of Jupiter has its own pest profile. Here’s how the pressure changes area to area — and what we watch for in each.

### Abacoa & the town center

Walkable neighborhoods and townhomes around Roger Dean Stadium and shops. Shared walls and density make German cockroaches, ants, and rodents the priority.

### Admirals Cove & the deep-water communities

Gated waterfront estates on canals with private docks. High humidity drives mosquitoes, no-see-ums, and roof rats traveling the seawalls.

### Jupiter Inlet & beachfront

Beachside homes and condos near the lighthouse and inlet. Salt air and dense landscaping favor ghost ants, white-footed ants, and roof rats.

### Loxahatchee River waterfront

Homes along the river and its creeks. Mosquitoes and no-see-ums off the water and roof rats along the shoreline and docks.

### Jupiter Farms

Large rural homesteads on acreage, well, and septic against the western wetlands. Wildlife, rodents around barns and outbuildings, fire ants, and heavy mosquito pressure.

Family neighborhoods with lakes and green space. Ants, rodents, and mosquitoes with termite protection for wood-frame and block homes.

### Jonathan’s Landing & gated golf communities

Golf and waterfront gated communities. Lawn and ornamental pests, mosquitoes off the lakes, and roof rats from mature landscaping.

### Central & older Jupiter neighborhoods

Established coastal homes with mature trees — drywood termites, ants, and humidity-driven pests.

## The Jupiter pest calendar: what to expect season by season

Pest pressure shifts through the year in South Florida. Here’s what tends to peak and when — so you stay ahead of it.

| Season | What peaks in Jupiter |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Termite swarm season — subterranean and drywood termites fly to start new colonies. The single most important time for a termite inspection. Ant and roach activity ramps up with the warmth. |
| Summer / Wet Season (Jun–Sep) | Peak mosquito and no-see-um pressure from daily rain and standing water. Palmetto bugs are highly active, rodents breed, and ant colonies hit full size — the hardest-hitting stretch of the year. |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | As the wet season winds down, rodents and roaches push indoors seeking shelter. Wildlife looks for attic harborage ahead of cooler nights. |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | South Florida’s mild winter means no true off-season — pests slow but never stop. Ideal window for exclusion work, attic sealing, and getting ahead of the spring surge. |
