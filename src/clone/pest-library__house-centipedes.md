# House Centipedes

Pest Library

## House Centipedes

Scutigera coleoptrata

The startling, fast-moving bug with what looks like a fringe of long legs darting across a bathroom wall — unnerving to see, but actually a predator that hunts the other pests in your home.

## How to identify them

House centipedes are unmistakable: a yellowish-gray body an inch or so long fringed with 15 pairs of very long, delicate legs, moving in a fast, almost flickering dash across walls and floors. Their speed and leggy silhouette make them one of the most alarming-looking household bugs, even though they are among the more harmless.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

House centipedes thrive in humidity and are found in the dampest parts of a home — bathrooms, laundry rooms, garages, and around plumbing. They are active hunters that prey on other household pests: roaches, silverfish, ants, spiders, and fly larvae.

Their presence is really a signal of two things — enough moisture to keep them comfortable and enough other insects to eat. They can deliver a minor bite if handled, but they are not aggressive and their bite is comparable to a mild bee sting at worst, and even that is rare.

## Signs of an infestation

- Fast, many-legged bugs darting across bathroom or garage walls
- Sightings concentrated in the most humid rooms
- Presence of other pests — their food — nearby
- Activity mostly at night when they hunt

## Why they are a problem

House centipedes are largely beneficial, so the issue is usually the startle factor rather than any real harm. That said, a persistent population points to underlying moisture and a supply of other pests, both of which are worth addressing. In numbers they become an unwelcome nuisance in living spaces.

## Preventing house centipedes

- Reduce humidity with ventilation and dehumidification in baths, laundry, and garages
- Fix leaks and eliminate standing water
- Control the other pests centipedes feed on
- Seal gaps around plumbing, doors, and the foundation
- Clear damp clutter and debris where they and their prey hide

## At a glance

- Fast mover
- Predator
- Moisture indicator
- Mostly harmless

### Quick facts

- Size: 1 to 1.5 inches (legs add more)
- Color: Yellowish-gray, dark stripes
- Legs: 15 pairs, very long
- Where: Damp baths, laundry, garages
- Bite: Rare, minor if handled
- Peak activity: Year-round in humid areas
