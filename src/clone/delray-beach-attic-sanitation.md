## Why a contaminated attic can’t just be left alone

Roof rats and wildlife are common in Delray Beach, and once they’ve been in an attic they leave behind droppings, urine, nesting debris, and often the ectoparasites (mites and fleas) they carried. In our warm, humid climate that contamination doesn’t simply dry up and disappear — it lingers as odor, degrades your insulation, and can pose a genuine health concern.

Beyond the health side, rodent activity mats down and soils insulation, destroying its R-value and driving up your cooling bills. Cleaning up properly — and pairing it with exclusion so nothing gets back in — restores both the safety and the efficiency of the space.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does a rodent-infested attic need to be sanitized?
Rodent droppings and urine can harbor pathogens and allergens, and the mess draws other pests. Sanitizing removes the contamination and neutralizes the health and odor issues rather than leaving them to linger in your attic air.

### Will sanitation get rid of the smell?
Yes — removing the contaminated material and then deodorizing is what eliminates the lingering rodent or wildlife odor, which a simple cleanup alone won’t fully resolve.

### Do I need to replace my attic insulation?
Often, if rodents have soiled and matted it. Contaminated insulation loses its R-value and holds odor, so we remove and replace it where needed — which also restores your energy efficiency.

### Should attic cleanup be done with rodent control?
Yes — sanitation and exclusion go together. Cleaning the attic while entry points are still open just invites re-contamination, so we coordinate the two for a lasting result.

### Is the cleanup safe for my household?
Yes. We use proper containment, HEPA vacuuming, and sanitizing methods to handle the contamination safely and keep it from spreading into your living space.

### Can this help with allergies or air quality?
It can — removing droppings, contaminated insulation, and the associated allergens and treating for left-behind parasites addresses a source of indoor irritants coming from the attic.
