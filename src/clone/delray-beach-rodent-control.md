## Why roof rats thrive in Delray Beach

The dominant rodent in Delray Beach is the roof rat — an agile climber that prefers to travel high, not on the ground. Our waterfront neighborhoods hand them a highway: seawalls and docks along the Intracoastal and the canals of Tropic Isle and the Marina District, dense landscaping, mature trees, and fences all lead straight to your roofline, soffits, and attic.

Add the older homes of Lake Ida and Del-Ida Park with their gaps and vents, plus the food sources around the Atlantic Avenue dining corridor, and you have ideal roof-rat conditions. Once inside, they gnaw wiring (a fire risk), contaminate insulation, and reproduce fast — which is why speed and sealing matter.

## Signs you have rodents

If you’re noticing any of these, it’s time for an inspection.

### Noises in the attic or walls

Scratching, scurrying, or gnawing sounds — especially at dusk and night — are the classic roof-rat giveaway.

### Droppings & grease marks

Dark droppings in the attic, garage, or pantry, and greasy smudge trails along walls and beams where rats travel.

### Gnaw marks & damage

Chewed wiring, insulation, stored boxes, and food packaging — roof rats gnaw constantly.

### Activity along the roofline

Rats seen running fence tops, tree limbs, or power lines toward the roof at dusk.
