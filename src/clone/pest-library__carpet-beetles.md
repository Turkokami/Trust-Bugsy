# Carpet Beetles

Pest Library

## Carpet Beetles

Anthrenus & Attagenus species

Tiny beetles whose bristly larvae quietly chew holes in wool, silk, and other natural fibers — often blamed on moths, and sometimes mistaken for bed bugs, but the damage is to your fabrics, not you.

## How to identify them

Adult carpet beetles are tiny, rounded beetles — mottled black, white, and orange, or solid dark — often found on window sills as they try to get outside. The damage, though, comes from the larvae: small, tan-to-brown, carrot-shaped grubs covered in bristly hairs (“woolly bears”) that hide in dark, undisturbed places.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Carpet beetle larvae feed on animal-based natural materials: wool, silk, fur, feathers, leather, felt, pet hair, lint, and even dead insects. They damage clothing, rugs, upholstery, blankets, taxidermy, and stored textiles, favoring closets, drawers, under furniture, and along baseboards where lint and hair collect.

Adults feed on pollen and nectar outdoors and fly to windows and lights, entering homes through gaps and on cut flowers, then laying eggs near suitable larval food. Because the larvae are secretive, damage often appears before the pest is noticed, and carpet beetles are frequently confused with clothes moths or even bed bugs.

## Signs of an infestation

- Irregular holes in wool, silk, or other natural-fiber items
- Bristly, carrot-shaped larvae or their shed skins in closets and drawers
- Small beetles on window sills trying to get out
- Damage in dark, undisturbed storage — not on people or bedding

## Why they are a problem

Carpet beetles can ruin valuable clothing, rugs, and heirloom textiles, and because the larvae work slowly in hidden spots, an infestation can cause significant damage before it is discovered. Their shed skins and hairs can also irritate skin in sensitive people, and they are easily mistaken for other pests, leading to the wrong treatment.

## How Bugsy’s treats carpet beetles

Control depends on locating the larval food sources — stored wool and silk, lint under furniture, pet hair, and dead-insect accumulations in wall voids and light fixtures. Our technicians inspect these harborage areas, treat them, and advise on cleaning and storage. Because dead insects and lint sustain them, we also address the conditions feeding the population.

## Preventing carpet beetles

- Store wool, silk, and fur items clean and sealed in airtight containers
- Vacuum closets, under furniture, and along baseboards regularly to remove lint and hair
- Clean up dead insects in light fixtures, window sills, and attics
- Inspect cut flowers and secondhand textiles before bringing them in
- Keep stored clothing periodically aired and disturbed rather than left untouched

### Quick facts

- Size: 1/16 to 1/8 inch
- Color: Mottled or solid dark; larvae bristly
- Damaging stage: Larvae (“woolly bears”)
- Damages: Wool, silk, fur, feathers, leather
- Adults: Feed on pollen; enter via windows
- Peak activity: Spring and summer
