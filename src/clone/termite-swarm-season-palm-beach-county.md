## Why swarm season is the warning sign you can’t ignore

Termites are active year-round in South Florida, but they “swarm” — release winged reproductives to start new colonies — mainly in the warm, wet months of spring and summer, often after rain. Subterranean termites (including the aggressive Formosan) tend to swarm in spring; drywood termites swarm through the warmer months. If you see swarmers or discarded wings on windowsills, it means a colony is mature enough to reproduce and is at or near your home.

The damage is slow, hidden, and expensive — termites eat wood from the inside out, and homeowners often don’t notice until floors, trim, or framing are already compromised. Because standard homeowners insurance typically does not cover termite damage, an inspection and a protection plan are far cheaper than a repair.

- Winged swarmers around windows, doors, and lights — indoors is a strong warning sign
- Small piles of discarded wings on sills or floors
- Mud tubes on foundation walls, piers, or in the garage (subterranean termites)
- Tiny pellet-like frass near wood (drywood termites)
- Hollow-sounding or blistered wood, and stuck or sagging floors

## The termites we treat in Palm Beach County

Different species need different treatment strategies, which is why an inspection comes first.

### Subterranean termites

Nest in the soil and enter through mud tubes; the most common and destructive type locally. Treated with soil/barrier treatments and, where appropriate, baiting systems.

### Formosan termites

An especially aggressive subterranean species with huge colonies — capable of serious damage fast. Requires prompt, professional treatment.

### Drywood termites

Live entirely inside the wood with no soil contact — common in attics, trim, and furniture. Treated with targeted or whole-structure approaches depending on extent.

### Why swarmers appear indoors

If swarmers emerge inside your home, the colony is likely already in the structure — this is the clearest signal to get inspected immediately.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is termite swarm season in Palm Beach County?
Subterranean termites typically swarm in spring, often on warm, humid days after rain, while drywood termites swarm through the warmer months into summer. In South Florida you can see activity nearly year-round, which is why any sign of swarmers warrants an inspection.

### I saw winged insects indoors — are they termites or flying ants?
They’re easy to confuse. Termite swarmers have straight antennae, equal-length wings, and a broad waist; flying ants have bent antennae, unequal wings, and a pinched waist. Either way, swarmers indoors are worth a professional look — bring us a few in a bag or a photo and we’ll identify them.

### How much damage can termites really do?
A lot, quietly. They eat wood from the inside out, and an established colony can compromise trim, flooring, and framing over months to years before it’s obvious. Because homeowners insurance generally excludes termite damage, prevention is dramatically cheaper than repair.

### Do I need treatment if I only see a few swarmers?
Very likely yes. Swarmers are reproductives leaving an existing, mature colony — seeing them (especially indoors) means the colony is nearby or already in the structure. A $99 inspection (applied to the cost of treatment) tells us the species and extent so we can right-size the treatment.

### Is termite treatment safe for my family and pets?
Yes, when performed by a licensed professional. We use EPA-registered products and modern application methods, apply them to the structure and soil rather than living space, and explain any re-entry guidance clearly.
