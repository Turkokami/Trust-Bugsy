# Smokybrown Cockroach

Pest Library

## Smokybrown Cockroach

Periplaneta fuliginosa

A large, glossy mahogany-brown roach that lives mostly outdoors in South Florida’s mulch, soffits, and screen enclosures — and flies to lights and slips indoors on warm, humid nights.

## How to identify them

The smokybrown is one of the large roaches South Floridians see most. Adults reach an inch to an inch and a half and are a uniform, shiny mahogany-to-dark brown all over — without the pale markings of the similar American or Australian roaches. Both sexes have fully developed wings and fly readily.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Unlike the German cockroach, the smokybrown is a peridomestic species — it lives primarily outdoors and wanders in. It loses body moisture quickly, so it seeks out humid harborage: mulch beds, leaf litter, tree holes, planters, wood piles, soffits, attics, and screen (pool) enclosures.

They are strong fliers and are drawn to lights, which is how they end up on porches, in garages, and inside through gaps and open doors on warm nights. They feed on decaying plant matter, and populations swell in the wet season.

Finding one indoors usually means good conditions outside near the structure — not necessarily interior filth.

## Signs of an infestation

- Large glossy brown roaches on porches, in garages, or flying to lights
- Activity in mulch, soffits, attics, and screen enclosures
- Droppings (small dark specks) in attics and along soffit lines
- Egg cases (dark, purse-shaped capsules) glued in sheltered outdoor spots

## Why they are a problem

Beyond the startle factor of a large flying roach, smokybrowns can carry bacteria across surfaces as they move between decaying matter and living spaces, and their droppings and shed skins can contribute to allergen load. Their strong flight and attraction to light make them persistent home invaders in the warm season.

## Preventing smokybrown cockroaches

- Keep mulch thin and pulled back from the foundation; clear leaf litter and debris
- Trim tree limbs and shrubs away from the roof and soffits
- Fix roof, gutter, and A/C moisture problems that create humid harborage
- Switch exterior bulbs to yellow “bug” lights and keep doors sealed at night
- Screen soffit and attic vents and seal gaps around eaves
