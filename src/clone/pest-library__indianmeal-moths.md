## Indianmeal Moths

Plodia interpunctella

## How to identify them

The Indianmeal moth is the most common pantry moth and is easy to recognize by its wings: the half nearest the body is pale gray and the outer half is a coppery, bronze color, split about the middle. Adults are small, roughly a half-inch across, and are usually first noticed fluttering around the kitchen, especially near the pantry or up at the ceiling.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

The adults do not feed or cause damage — the caterpillars do. Larvae infest a wide range of stored dry goods: flour, cereal, grains, crackers, dried fruit, nuts, spices, birdseed, and dry pet food. As they feed they spin silk, leaving webbing, clumping, and their droppings in the product.

When mature, the larvae wander away from the food to pupate, which is why you often find caterpillars or cocoons on pantry ceilings, shelf edges, and where the wall meets the ceiling — far from the infested package. A single overlooked source, like an old bag of birdseed, can reseed the pantry repeatedly.

## Signs of an infestation

- Small two-tone moths flying around the kitchen and pantry
- Webbing, clumping, or silk in flour, cereal, grains, or pet food
- Caterpillars or cocoons on pantry ceilings, shelves, and corners
- Recurring moths despite killing the ones you see

## Why they are a problem

Indianmeal moths contaminate stored food with webbing, cast skins, and droppings, forcing you to discard infested items — and because they spread from package to package, one missed source keeps the problem alive. They do not bite or damage the structure, but the contamination and persistence make them a serious pantry nuisance.

## Preventing Indianmeal moths

- Store grains, cereal, pet food, and birdseed in sealed hard containers
- Check and rotate pantry stock; discard old or expired dry goods
- Clean shelf cracks and corners where crumbs and larvae collect
- Inspect new packages — infestations often arrive from the store
- Address the source promptly rather than just swatting the moths
