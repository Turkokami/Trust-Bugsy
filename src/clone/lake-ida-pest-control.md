## Why Lake Ida’s setting drives pest pressure

Lake Ida sits northwest of downtown around its namesake freshwater lake, and it’s defined by generous lots, a dense canopy of live oaks and ficus, and a mix of original mid-century ranch homes alongside newer custom estates. It’s a wonderful place to live — and every one of those features is also a pest driver.

The mature tree canopy is a highway for roof rats, whose limbs and branches lead straight to roofs, soffits, and attics. Older wood-frame and slab homes give both subterranean and drywood termites easy footholds, and Lake Ida’s many original structures are prime territory for the drywood termites that live inside dry framing. The lake and heavy irrigation of these big lawns keep mosquitoes and no-see-ums breeding, and the expansive St. Augustine turf invites chinch bugs and lawn pests. A Lake Ida program has to address all of it.
