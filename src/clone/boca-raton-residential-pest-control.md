## Why Boca Raton homes face year-round pest pressure

Boca Raton stretches from the Atlantic barrier island and the Intracoastal west across canals, golf-course lakes, and the El Rio corridor — and that varied, water-rich geography is exactly what keeps pests moving all twelve months.

Boca Raton runs from the beach and the Intracoastal Waterway on the east, through the dense downtown around Mizner Park, out to the sprawling gated golf communities and family neighborhoods of West Boca. All of it sits in a humid subtropical climate that never really gets a killing frost, so pests here don’t take a winter off — they breed, forage, and move into homes year-round.

Water is the common thread. The Intracoastal, Lake Boca Raton, the El Rio Canal, and the countless lakes threaded through communities like Boca West, Broken Sound, and Woodfield keep humidity high and draw mosquitoes, no-see-ums, and roof rats that travel seawalls, fences, and tree lines straight to the roofline. Downtown around Mizner Park and along Federal Highway, dense condos and a busy restaurant scene create steady food and harborage that push cockroaches and rodents into nearby homes. And East Boca’s older Golden Triangle homes give drywood termites decades of undisturbed structural wood to work in.

The upshot: effective pest control in Boca Raton isn’t a one-size-fits-all spray. It’s built around where your home sits, how it’s built, and which pests your specific neighborhood actually deals with — the way Bugsy’s approaches every Boca property.

## The Boca Raton pest calendar: what to expect season by season

Pest pressure shifts through the year in South Florida. Here’s what tends to peak and when — so you stay ahead of it.

| Season | What peaks in Boca Raton |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Termite swarm season — subterranean and drywood termites fly to start new colonies. The single most important time for a termite inspection. Ant and roach activity ramps up with the warmth. |
| Summer / Wet Season (Jun–Sep) | Peak mosquito and no-see-um pressure from daily rain and standing water. Palmetto bugs are highly active, rodents breed, and ant colonies hit full size — the hardest-hitting stretch of the year. |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | As the wet season winds down, rodents and roaches push indoors seeking shelter. Wildlife looks for attic harborage ahead of cooler nights. |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | South Florida’s mild winter means no true off-season — pests slow but never stop. Ideal window for exclusion work, attic sealing, and getting ahead of the spring surge. |
