## Why Wellington is a mosquito hot spot

Mosquitoes need only a bottle-cap of standing water to breed — and Wellington is surrounded by it. the C-51 and LWDD canals, hundreds of community and equestrian lakes, and the western wetlands all create prime breeding habitat, and the daily rain of our May-to-October wet season refills every low spot, planter, and gutter in between.

On top of mosquitoes, the area deals with **no-see-ums** (biting midges) — tiny, vicious biters that come off the water at dawn and dusk and slip through standard screens. Left untreated, the combination can make a beautiful yard, patio, or dock unusable for months.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often do you treat for mosquitoes?
Through Wellington’s wet season, a monthly recurring barrier treatment is typical — it keeps the population knocked down as rain constantly refills breeding sites.

### Are mosquito treatments safe for my pets and pollinators?
We apply with pet and pollinator safety in mind — targeting resting areas and breeding water, timing applications appropriately, and avoiding blooming plants where practical.

### Can you do anything about no-see-ums?
Yes. Wellington gets significant no-see-um (biting midge) pressure off the water, and we include treatment targeted at them as part of our program.

### Do I need to be home for the treatment?
No — most mosquito service is performed on the exterior, so you don’t need to be home. We’ll let you know when we’ve been out.

### Will one treatment get rid of my mosquitoes?
A single treatment provides relief, but mosquitoes are constantly re-breeding, so recurring service through the wet season is what keeps your yard usable all summer.

### What can I do to help between treatments?
Dump standing water weekly — planters, saucers, bird baths, tarps, and clogged gutters — and keep bromeliads flushed. Removing breeding water is the single most effective thing a homeowner can do.
