## Who’s responsible — and where we fit

Florida law and your governing documents split pest-control responsibility between the association and individual owners. Knowing the line keeps boards compliant and keeps disputes off the agenda.

Association responsibility

### Common elements & shared areas

- Building exteriors, structural components, and shared systems (condos, Ch. 718)
- Clubhouses, pools, fitness rooms, hallways, elevators & mail areas
- Landscaping, sidewalks, and grounds (HOAs, Ch. 720)
- Dumpster corrals, trash chutes, and utility / mechanical rooms
- Any infestation that originates in a common area or spreads across shared walls to multiple units

Owner responsibility

### Inside the individual unit

- Pests confined to a single unit’s interior
- Housekeeping-driven activity within a home
- Owner-added structures or improvements
- We offer owners discounted in-unit plans that dovetail with the community program

General guidance under Fla. Stat. Ch. 718 (condominiums) and Ch. 720 (homeowners’ associations). Your declaration and bylaws control — we help boards map coverage to their specific documents.

## What condo boards should ask a pest control company

Before signing a pest control contract, a condo or HOA board should ask who is responsible for common areas versus individual units, how unit-level problems like roaches get handled building-wide, what termite and WDO coverage includes, and what written service reports the board will receive after every visit.

- Are you licensed and insured in Florida, and can you provide certificates for the association’s files?
- How do you handle a roach or ant problem that spreads between units?
- What is included in common-area service, and how often?
- How will you coordinate unit access and resident notices with management?
- What termite and wood-destroying organism (WDO) coverage do you offer for the building?
- What written reports will the board receive after each visit?
