# Cicada Killer Wasps

Pest Library

## Cicada Killer Wasps

Sphecius speciosus

Startlingly large solitary wasps that dig burrows in bare, sandy soil and lawns — alarming to look at, but the hovering males can’t sting and the females almost never do.

## How to identify them

Cicada killers are among the largest wasps in Florida — up to nearly two inches — black with yellow markings and amber wings. Their size makes them frightening, but their behavior is telling: males patrol and hover around burrows and territories, while females fly low over the ground hunting cicadas. The clearest sign is a burrow: a finger-sized hole with a fan of excavated soil, often in bare, sandy, sunny spots.

## Behavior and biology in South Florida

Cicada killers are solitary. Each female digs her own burrow in loose, well-drained soil — lawns with thin turf, flower beds, slopes, sidewalk edges, and sandy patches — then hunts cicadas, paralyzes them, and stocks the burrow as food for her larvae.

Despite their intimidating size and the males’ habit of dive-bombing anything that moves through their territory, males have no stinger at all, and females are docile, using their sting to subdue cicadas rather than to defend. Stings to people are very rare and usually only occur if a female is grabbed or stepped on barefoot.

## Signs of an infestation

- Large wasps hovering low over the lawn or patrolling a sunny area
- Finger-sized burrow holes with mounded or fan-shaped soil
- Activity concentrated in thin-turf, sandy, or bare sunny spots
- Repeated burrows in the same beds or lawn edges each summer

## Why they are a problem

Cicada killers pose little sting risk, so they are mainly a nuisance of appearance and lawn cosmetics: their size frightens people, and their burrowing leaves scattered soil mounds and can worsen thin, bare turf. Large numbers in a play area or garden are unsettling even though the risk is low.

## Preventing cicada killer wasps

- Thicken thin or bare turf — dense grass discourages burrowing
- Mulch or plant bare, sandy patches they favor
- Water and maintain the lawn to reduce dry, loose soil
- Address burrows in play areas even though the sting risk is low
- Avoid swatting hovering males — they cannot sting
